### ~10:15pm PDT — Optimal P/E band study (Jake's ask), run on the index
Stock-level forward-P/E history needs IBES/FactSet — not free, and yfinance only has *current* forward EPS, so any stock "history" built from it is look-ahead contaminated. Ran the honest available version: **152 years of the index (Shiller 1871-2023 + multpl to 9/4/26), trailing P/E → forward 12m real total return, with a control row and era splits.** Wrote the stock-level piece as a Colab cell (`tools/fwd_pe_band_screen_cell.py`) that does the current cross-section + two bands + trap flags, and says in its header what it does NOT do.

**Three findings.** (1) **The optimal band exists — trailing 10-12×, +15.7% vs control +8.6%, 83% hit — and has ZERO post-1990 months.** The modern market has not traded below 14× trailing for a generation; the era split exposed it. (2) **Today's 26.4× trailing is in the 25-30 bucket: mean +1.0%, hit 51%, 5y annualised +2.1%** — but the mean is ~0 because the bucket averages the month before an earnings collapse (2000, 2008, 2021) with the month after it (2002, 2003, 2009, 2020). Trailing P/E hits 25-30 at BOTH price peaks and earnings troughs; it can't tell them apart. **26.4× is not a crash call; it's a regime where the outcome depends on the earnings path.** (3) **CAPE is the cleaner axis and it's the one that goes negative: ≥35 → −3.7% mean, 43% hit** (1999-2000, 2021). Current CAPE ⬜ — the Yale file stops 9/2023 and shillerdata.com is blocked here.

**The translation to Jake's question:** headline fwd 20.0× vs trailing 26.4× ⇒ the market prices ~32% forward EPS growth. The index is "18-20× forward" (+14.5% post-1990) **only if that lands** — and every thread this week (memory margins, flat revenue/GW, CCC, hyperscaler spreads) is a bet on the composition of that estimate. Headline ≠ composition, applied to earnings.
