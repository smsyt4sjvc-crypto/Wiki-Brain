G20: G20 INNOVATION MINISTERIAL — CHAPEL HILL
SEPT. 1–2, 2026 | 2-DAY SUMMARY

DAY 1 — SEPT. 1

• ELON MUSK — POWER IS THE NEXT AI BOTTLENECK
  - Warned of a significant AI-power shortfall in 2027, citing ~15 GW as a possible deficit.
  - Said AI-chip production is growing ~40–50% annually versus only ~10–20% growth in available power outside China.
  - Urged countries to rapidly add generation to attract AI data centers.
  - Said his companies are already building generation alongside data centers.
  - Explicitly linked GPU export controls to geography: China has enormous power availability but cannot freely access the newest U.S. accelerators.

  MARKET READ:
  AI constraint is moving downstream:
  GPUs → electricity → turbines → blades/vanes → transformers → switchgear → construction.
  Bullish thesis: GEV, BE, HWM, ETN, PWR; longer term CEG/BWXT/OKLO.

• DAVID SACKS — “BUILD YOUR OWN POWER”
  - Estimated U.S. AI-related infrastructure investment at roughly $800B in 2026 and potentially ~$1.4T in 2027.
  - Compared the buildout with the scale of the railroad boom.
  - Explicitly supported behind-the-meter generation.
  - Argued data centers should add net-new power instead of forcing utilities/ratepayers to absorb their incremental load.
  - Suggested surplus private generation could eventually feed the grid and support grid upgrades.

  MARKET READ:
  Behind-the-meter power is moving from an engineering workaround toward preferred policy architecture.

• AI REGULATION — CAROLINA PRINCIPLES
  - U.S. pushed a light-touch AI framework:
      • No “FDA for AI” requiring preapproval of new models.
      • Use existing fraud/privacy/discrimination/copyright/product-liability law where possible.
      • Add AI-specific rules mainly where genuinely new risks arise.
      • Favor disclosures/testing/auditing over licensing.
  - China reportedly signed onto the principles.
  - Michael Kratsios held a bilateral meeting with Chinese Science & Technology Minister Yin Hejun.
  - No chip/export-control concession emerged from that meeting.

• MUSK VS. EUROPE
  - Musk argued technology should be “default legal” rather than “default illegal.”
  - Directly criticized Europe’s heavy AI regulatory model.
  - Reinforced a growing split:
      U.S. → permissionless/light-touch deployment
      EU → ex-ante regulation/enforcement

• DATA-CENTER LABOR BOTTLENECK
  - Mark Zuckerberg said hyperscalers are struggling to find enough electricians, pipefitters and other skilled tradespeople to build planned data centers.
  - Adds LABOR/CONSTRUCTION CAPACITY to GPUs and electricity as a major physical constraint.


DAY 2 — SEPT. 2

• HOWARD LUTNICK — DIRECTIONALLY LIGHTER AI/TECH POLICY
  - Said the administration’s inclination toward AI releases/imports/exports is “the opposite” of a heavier regulatory hand.
  - Important because Commerce controls BIS and semiconductor export policy.
  - Not yet a formal relaxation of Nvidia/China controls.

• JENSEN HUANG — GOVERNMENTS SHOULD BUILD AI ECONOMIES
  - Backed regulating actual demonstrated harms rather than theoretical AI harms.
  - Encouraged countries to accelerate domestic AI infrastructure and build their own AI economies.
  - Effectively reinforced the “sovereign AI” thesis:
      local GPUs + local data centers + local power infrastructure.
  - Said he had not discussed additional semiconductor tariffs with officials at the event.

  MARKET READ:
  Global AI capex can broaden beyond U.S. hyperscalers into sovereign/national infrastructure spending.

• EXPORT CONTROLS REMAIN THE BIG UNRESOLVED ISSUE
  - Washington still needs to determine what ultimately replaces the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule.
  - This is materially more important to NVDA than the nonbinding regulatory principles.
  - No new China GPU concession has been announced.

• SAM ALTMAN
  - Scheduled for Day 2 with Lutnick.
  - As of the latest check, no comparably meaningful new Altman policy announcement, financing deal or infrastructure commitment had surfaced.


BOTTOM LINE

The dominant message from Chapel Hill was not merely “AI is growing.”

It was:

AI CAPEX IS BECOMING PHYSICAL INFRASTRUCTURE POLICY.

The emerging architecture is:

AI campus
   ↓
bring/build/buy dedicated power
   ↓
behind-the-meter generation
   ↓
gas turbines / fuel cells immediately
   ↓
batteries + private substations + microgrids
   ↓
closed-loop liquid cooling
   ↓
eventually nuclear / SMRs
   ↓
potential surplus generation returned to the grid

The most important investable shift is that AI infrastructure spending is broadening from:

NVDA / GPUs

to:

POWER GENERATION + TURBINES + TURBINE COMPONENTS + ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT + GRID CONSTRUCTION + NUCLEAR.

Our concentrated AI-energy expressions:
BE / GEV / TLN / HWM / OKLO

More conservative infrastructure additions:
ETN / PWR / CEG / BWXT.
