Thesis: Power is the bottleneck, but chips are the cleaner way to monetize the solution

AI demand is not the problem. The constraint is increasingly power. The issue is that the power buildout is
fragmented across too many technologies and too many sources of execution risk: grid upgrades, transmission,
transformers, gas turbines, nuclear, geothermal, solar/storage, behind-the-meter generation, permitting, land,
labor, tariffs, financing, construction delays, politics and NIMBY.

That makes the energy side difficult to underwrite cleanly. A dollar of power capex can disappear into years of
engineering, permitting, labor, interconnection and financing before it produces a single monetizable MWh. Even
when a project succeeds, the equity return depends heavily on structure: regulated utility, merchant generation,
PPA, tax credits, capacity payments, behind-the-meter economics, etc.

Semiconductors are much cleaner. For AMD, Nvidia, SK hynix, Micron, Broadcom, Marvell, TSMC, Semtech and the
equipment names, the economic chain is far easier to model: known chip/input cost -> known ASP -> known volume
-> known gross margin -> known economic output.

The semiconductor supplier does not particularly care whether the marginal electron came from a nuclear plant, a
GE Vernova turbine, geothermal, solar + storage, a utility grid expansion or an xAI/SpaceX behind-the-meter
plant. Once the power becomes available, it ultimately gets converted into: MW/GW -> racks -> accelerators/ASICs
-> HBM -> networking -> storage -> packaging.

That is the key asymmetry: Power capex tells you that more silicon will eventually be needed, but it does not
necessarily tell you which power company will earn the best return.

THE POWER BOTTLENECK DOES NOT INVALIDATE THE CHIP THESIS
A power shortage only becomes bearish for semiconductors if AI customers decide they no longer want the compute.
That is not what is happening. Instead, hyperscalers, AI labs, governments and infrastructure developers are
spending enormous amounts to solve the power bottleneck. The constraint is causing more investment, not less
demand.

So the sequence is: AI demand rises -> compute demand rises -> power becomes the bottleneck -> capital floods
into every possible power solution -> power capacity becomes credible/de-risked -> customers commit GPU/HBM/
networking orders -> semiconductor cycle accelerates -> capacity eventually energizes and compute revenue begins.

The important timing point is that semis should lead physical energization. You do not wait until the power plant
or data center turns on. The investable signal is when the power becomes financeable and believable:
* land secured * interconnection approved * PPA signed * turbines/transformers ordered * financing closed
* construction visibly underway * credible energization date established * hyperscaler reserves the capacity
At that point, the future silicon demand tranche starts becoming underwritable.

"CYCLICAL POWER = CYCLICAL CHIPS"
Power will not arrive smoothly. It will come online in waves. That creates the possibility of recurring
semiconductor re-rating cycles: power bottleneck -> semis correct because deployment appears constrained -> new
power tranche gets de-risked -> GPU/HBM/networking orders accelerate -> semi earnings estimates rise -> stocks
re-rate sharply -> positioning gets crowded / expectations peak -> another correction -> next power tranche
begins. That is essentially the framework behind the kind of February-June semiconductor run you were describing.
The semiconductor cycle becomes partly a power-capacity visibility cycle.

WHY THIS MAY BE A BUYING OPPORTUNITY
The interesting setup is when semi prices/positioning correct while AI capex, server demand, HBM demand,
packaging expansion, foundry utilization and future power capacity continue moving higher. That divergence is
more interesting than simply saying "SOX is down." The thesis is strongest when the market temporarily interprets
power scarcity as a cap on chip demand, while the real-world response is actually: spend hundreds of billions to
solve power scarcity so that even more chips can be deployed. That turns the power constraint into a delayed
semiconductor demand pool, not necessarily destroyed demand.

WHY SEMIS MAY BE CLEANER THAN THE POWER BASKET
The power winners are difficult to know in advance because the solution is scattered. You could be right that AI
needs vastly more electricity and still pick the wrong: utility, nuclear developer, turbine manufacturer,
geothermal company, solar/storage company, transmission owner, data-center power developer. But almost every
successful solution eventually terminates in more powered silicon. That makes semiconductors the more
concentrated expression of the same infrastructure thesis.

BEST SEMICONDUCTOR EXPOSURES
Accelerators: Nvidia, AMD. HBM/memory: SK hynix, Micron, Samsung. Custom silicon + networking: Broadcom, Marvell.
Foundry/advanced packaging: TSMC. Semiconductor equipment: ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA.
Connectivity/signal integrity: Semtech.
The equipment names are especially useful because they do not require you to know whether Nvidia, AMD, Google
TPU, AWS Trainium or another ASIC ultimately wins. More advanced semiconductor capacity requires more equipment.

PORTFOLIO FRAMEWORK WE ARRIVED AT
Rather than putting everything into semiconductors immediately, the idea was to use a long-duration
infrastructure base plus tactical semiconductor dry powder. Conceptually:
50-60% power/infrastructure bottleneck exposure / 15-20% permanent semiconductor core / 25-30% cash/dry powder
for semiconductor-cycle dislocations.
Or, in the simpler version you proposed: ~70% diversified power/infrastructure basket / ~30% cash available to
deploy into semiconductor cycles.
The important modification is that the 70% should probably not be only energy producers. It can include the
unavoidable infrastructure bottlenecks: turbines, transformers, switchgear, grid equipment, transmission,
nuclear, behind-the-meter generation, cooling, data-center infrastructure. Then the tactical 30% gets deployed
when the next power-to-silicon tranche becomes visible.

WHAT TO TRACK
newly de-risked MW/GW of AI power * credible energization dates * hyperscaler capex revisions * GPU/ASIC orders
* HBM contract pricing * HBM capacity * TSMC utilization * advanced packaging / CoWoS capacity * networking
demand * semiconductor lead times * inventories * DSO * gross margins * fab-equipment orders * turbine/
transformer delivery schedules * PPAs * utility/interconnection approvals * financing closes * project delays
* semiconductor positioning/volatility

The weekly task we created is designed around exactly this question: Is the next power tranche becoming credible
enough to create the next semiconductor demand and re-rating cycle?

BOTTOM LINE
AI demand is so strong that power has become the bottleneck. Power solutions are fragmented, capital-intensive
and difficult to underwrite. But nearly every successful power solution ultimately enables more high-margin
semiconductor deployment. Therefore, power-capacity de-risking can be used as a leading indicator for
semiconductor demand cycles, allowing you to keep long-duration exposure to the infrastructure buildout while
tactically buying semis during periods when their prices temporarily disconnect from the underlying expansion in
future powered compute.

Or even shorter: Power is the bottleneck. Power investment is scattered. Silicon is the common endpoint. Buy the
infrastructure buildout, then use each newly de-risked power wave to time the next chip cycle.
