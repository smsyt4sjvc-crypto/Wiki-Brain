Broad credit remained extremely tight: IG OAS 79 bp, BBB 97 bp, HY 260 bp, and CCC/lower 1,026 bp, while effective yields were already 5.44% for total IG and 6.18% for 15Y+ IG before today’s further Treasury selloff. That is why the absence of spread widening remains the most important missing confirmation of systemic stress.

Treasury’s next long-end test comes next week: the regular schedule calls for the 3-year on Sept. 8, 10-year reopening on Sept. 9, and 30-year reopening on Sept. 10. Separately, Treasury’s enlarged long-end liquidity-support buybacks begin Sept. 9, with operation limits rising from $2B to at least $4B in both the 10Y–20Y and 20Y–30Y sectors. 

Corporate supply is adding directly to that competition for duration: August IG issuance hit a record $164B, heavily influenced by AI investment, and BofA currently expects roughly $190B more in September. 

The most important new shadow-leverage disclosure is the SB Energy IPO filing. Nvidia’s SEC filings confirm a $105B maximum PORTS-Pike residual-value guarantee, plus another $3.5B of AI-cloud guarantees, for $108.5B maximum gross exposure; the obligations phase in as facilities enter service and decline as OpenAI performs under the leases. Nvidia reports only $56.6B of cash, equivalents and marketable debt securities, although OpenAI has agreed to indemnify Nvidia for amounts actually paid under the guarantee. 

SB Energy’s now-public structure makes the capital chain unusually visible: Nvidia is committing $3B of equity/prepaid-forward capital, OpenAI holds roughly 4 million warrants, and SB Energy has about 8.8 GW of contracted future data-center capacity despite essentially no operating data-center capacity today. Separately, Anthropic’s newly reported $35B Lambda compute agreement uses a Hut 8 Texas development where Nvidia reportedly holds the underlying lease—another direct example of Nvidia’s credit/lease position helping bridge the developer, cloud operator and AI customer.
