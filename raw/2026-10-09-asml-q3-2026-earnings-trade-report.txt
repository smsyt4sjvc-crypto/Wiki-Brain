S E C T I O N  1
The earnings engine, in plain language
ASML sells the machines that print the smallest features on advanced chips (lithography systems), plus the
services and upgrades that keep an installed fleet running and improving. Three pillars turn that activity into
earnings. Each pillar answers a different question about time: what fabs have committed to, what ASML
shipped this quarter, and what the existing fleet earns every quarter regardless of new orders.
PILLAR A: COMMITMENTS
Customer commitments, order intake, and backlog tell you what fabs will need years from
now
When a chipmaker plans a new fab or a capacity expansion, it commits to lithography tools long before
silicon ships. Those commitments become ASML order intake, and unshipped orders accumulate as
backlog. A large, durable backlog does two earnings jobs: it makes future system revenue more
predictable, and it justifies ASML investing in its own tool capacity ahead of demand.
Backlog stood at EUR 38.797B at end 2025.[4] ASML stopped disclosing quarterly orders after Q4 2025.
The last quarterly prints were Q3 2025 net bookings of EUR 5.399B (EUR 3.6B EUV) and Q4 2025 net
bookings of EUR 13.158B (EUR 7.4B EUV), an all time high.[4][6] For the first half of 2026, intake is
described only qualitatively as “extremely strong”, with 2027 low NA EUV described as “close to fully
covered with orders”.[5][7]
The capacity plan is the commitment made visible: plus 30% low NA EUV output (about 65 to about 85
systems) and plus 30% DUV immersion (about 130 to about 170) in 2027, with another plus 30%
under study for 2028, all within the existing footprint.[5][7] Management presents this as a demand
durability signal funded by confirmed order coverage. Read literally, ASML is spending to build more tools
because customers have already committed to fill the capacity.
PILLAR B: SHIPMENTS AND MIX
EUV system shipments and mix set the quarter’s system revenue and its profitability
Commitments become earnings when systems ship and are recognized as sales. EUV systems carry the
highest average selling prices, so the EUV share of system sales is the single biggest mix lever on both
revenue and gross margin. A quarter with more EUV in the mix is normally a richer quarter, even at the
same unit count.
In Q2 2026 (reported July 15, 2026), ASML sold 86 new systems, with net system sales of EUR 6.6B:
EUV EUR 3.8B including one High NA system, and non EUV EUR 2.8B. The end use split was logic
51% and memory 49%.[5] The 2026 plan calls for about 65 low NA EUV systems and about 130
immersion DUV systems.[5] Two things to hold onto: EUV mix is the average selling price and margin
driver, and memory at nearly half of system sales in Q2 2026 means a memory slowdown would show up
fast in this pillar.

PILLAR C: INSTALLED BASE AND MARGIN
Installed Base Management (services and upgrades) and gross margin
Every system ASML has ever shipped needs service, spare parts, and periodic upgrades. That Installed
Base Management (IBM) revenue recurs whether new orders are strong or soft, and it tends to carry
attractive margins. When IBM runs ahead of guidance, it can lift total gross margin even in a mixed
shipment quarter, which is exactly what happened in Q2 2026.
IBM sales were EUR 2.762B in Q2 2026, about EUR 300M above guidance, and drove the gross margin
beat to 54.0%.[5] Q3 2026 guidance points to IBM of about EUR 2.9B with gross margin of 55% to 57%.
Full year 2026 guidance was raised at the Q2 2026 call to sales of EUR 43B to 45B (from EUR 36B to
40B) with gross margin of 54% to 56%.[5][7]

How the three pillars combine
Commitments (Pillar A) say whether future system revenue exists. Shipments and mix (Pillar B) say how
much of it lands this quarter and at what richness. Installed Base Management (Pillar C) smooths the path
and, lately, has been the margin surprise. An earnings print can therefore beat on the quarter and still
disappoint, if the commitment language underneath it softens. That pattern matters for October 14, and
Section 3 shows it in the stock.

Trade structure analysis: mechanics, hurdles, and risks
Analytical only. This section describes how structures behave around the print. It does not recommend any position, size, or direction.
Directional long premium: long call or long put
A long call profits if the stock moves up past the premium paid by expiry, and a long put profits on the
equivalent down move. Both carry the same structural features into an earnings print:
The hurdle is the implied move. With implied at plus or minus 8.0%, a directional long must see the stock
move beyond roughly that magnitude in the chosen direction, after costs, before expiry on October 16.
IV crush hits both directions. The October 16 IV (about 57.7%) embeds an earnings premium over
November (about 43.5% to 44.2%). When the print clears, that premium normally collapses toward the post
event level on the morning after. A correct directional call can still lose money if the move is smaller than the
premium deflation.
Theta decay runs into the event. Premium paid days early decays while the binary event is still ahead.
Pricing evolves as uncertainty resolves closer to the print, so entry timing changes the effective price of the
same view.
Long volatility: long straddle or long strangle
A long straddle (at the money call plus put) or strangle (out of the money pair) profits if the realized move
exceeds the implied plus or minus 8.0% in either direction. It benefits when the IV premium is mispriced relative
to the volatility that actually arrives, and it is direction neutral, which suits a print where Section 2 argues the
surprise could come from language rather than numbers. The offsetting mechanics are severe: IV crush after
the print means the position normally must be entered cheap enough and exited promptly, and most long
volatility positions around earnings are exited the day of or the day after the print rather than held through
remaining decay.

Scenario math and the risks that dominate
Illustrative straddle mechanics (not a recommendation)
With the implied move at about plus or minus $142 on the October 9 intraday price of $1,783.02, a long at
the money straddle needs the stock roughly outside $1,641 to $1,925 by the October 16 expiry to cover
the illustrated move at expiry, before accounting for the actual premium quoted at entry, bid and ask
spreads, and the post print IV level at exit. Inside that band at expiry, the illustrated straddle loses value
even if the earnings headline reads well or badly. These are mechanics for reading a quote, stated as
arithmetic, not as an entry instruction.
Key risks common to long premium structures here
Inside the band losses: the stock can drift inside plus or minus 8.0% on a print the headlines call good or
bad, and long premium loses on both directional and straddle structures. Recent history (three straight
prints inside plus or minus 2.5%) is exactly that scenario.
Liquidity and wide spreads in the earnings expiry can make the quoted premium meaningfully worse than
midpoint arithmetic suggests.
Gap risk beyond the implied move: October 2024 (-15.6% day 0) shows the distribution has a tail the
implied move does not cap. That tail helps long volatility and hurts any short premium construction, which
this report does not analyze in depth because the brief is framed around catching the reaction with defined
risk long premium.
Exit timing dominates: for long premium, the exit decision on the morning after the print (sell into the
crush, or hold for a follow through move) typically matters more than small differences in entry premium.

S E C T I O N  7
Financing health check: verdict clean
The question in this section is narrow: is there anything in ASML’s recent financing that should worry a reader
holding exposure through earnings, such as new borrowing to fund buybacks, convertible dilution overhangs, or
accounting stress? On the filings reviewed, the answer is no.
Filings reviewed: the FY2025 Form 20-F filed February 25, 2026, and all 2026 Forms 6-K.[13][14]
No new long term bonds were issued in 2025 or 2026. The EUR 10B EMTN program established March
20, 2026 is an unused shelf. FY2025 debt actually fell by about EUR 400M as the EUR 1B 3.5% bond
matured on December 6, 2025.[13][14]
No convertibles, no restatements, no going concern language. PwC gave an unqualified opinion after a
planned KPMG to PwC rotation under EU audit rules.[13]
Capital returns were funded, not borrowed: EUR 5.95B of FY2025 buybacks plus EUR 2.55B of
dividends were paid from EUR 13.8B of operating cash flow while debt declined. That is the opposite of a
borrow to buyback pattern.[13]
Related party flows are disclosed and directional: Carl Zeiss flows (EUR 4.4B of purchases, EUR 1.9B
of loans receivable) are long standing, secured by parental guarantee, and flow toward the supplier for
ASML’s own optics roadmap.[13]
Maturity profile is laddered: EUR 4.46B total across 2026 to 2032, all fixed rate, plus a EUR 1.5B
undrawn committed facility to 2030. The 2027 plus 30% EUV and DUV capacity expansion is funded from
operating cash flow per stated financing policy.[13][14]
Method caveat and one unverified item. Filings were reviewed via full text filing mirrors (StockTitan) cross checked against filing
trackers, after direct EDGAR access returned errors from the research environment. No figures were invented to fill gaps. One item
