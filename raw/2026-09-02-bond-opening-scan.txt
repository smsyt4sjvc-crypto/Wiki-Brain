The latest official Fed H.15 observations put 5Y/10Y/30Y real Treasury yields at 2.18%/2.44%/2.99%, with EFFR at 3.63%. The jump in nominal yields relative to those real yields is consistent with 5Y/10Y breakevens moving to roughly 2.37%/2.35% on September 1. 

The ICE BofA data in the report are prior-close daily index observations, not live opening spreads. The notable change is still small at investment grade—BBB OAS was 98 bp on Aug. 31 versus 97 bp previously—while the weakest credit tail moved more materially. 

Corporate supply remains an important competing source of duration: U.S. investment-grade borrowers issued a record $164 billion in August, and BofA currently expects about $190 billion in September despite the jump in Treasury yields.  Treasury’s enlarged long-end liquidity-support buybacks begin September 9, increasing the maximum from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation in both the 10Y-20Y and 20Y-30Y sectors. 

The most consequential accounting detail in this run comes directly from CoreWeave’s Q2 10-Q. CoreWeave says it reassessed a data-center service provider as a VIE during Q2 because changes affected the adequacy of the provider’s equity at risk, but still determined it was not the primary beneficiary and therefore did not consolidate it. Its remaining completion-funding commitment has no contractual cap, although CoreWeave currently estimates the obligation as immaterial.  Its two other data-center-development VIE commitments reached $1.7 billion, and it continues to classify the $1.8 billion equipment-lessor structure as unconsolidated. 

Oracle’s filing makes September particularly relevant: its approximately $260 billion uncommenced lease stack includes a lease whose lessor borrowing Oracle guarantees up to $3.3 billion, maturing in September 2026. Its data-center power/other obligations were $13.309 billion, with another $19 billion of cloud-infrastructure commitments entered after year-end. 

SpaceX’s filings continue to provide the clearest demonstration of the accounting-substance mechanism: Valor AI-equipment transactions were deemed failed sale-leasebacks, resulting in $2.039 billion current plus $11.290 billion noncurrent debt at June 30. Total “other financings” were $13.406 billion, while Q2 AI-segment capital expenditures were $15.828 billion. 

Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft and Tesla’s latest primary filings continue to support the existing Level 0-2 classifications: Meta reports $278.99 billion of uncommenced leases and $349.31 billion of contractual commitments; Alphabet reports $85.2 billion of uncommenced leases, $43.8 billion of credit-derivative backstops and $7.6 billion of financial guarantees; Microsoft reports the enormous datacenter lease stack without an equivalent disclosed off-balance-sheet VIE issue; and Tesla’s direct Musk-AI exposure remains primarily its $2 billion SpaceX investment plus $405 million of H1 SpaceX-related Megapack revenue.
