1. The earnings engine, in plain language
TSMC makes almost all of its money by manufacturing chips designed by other companies. Three things decide how
much it earns: what kind of chips customers order, how full and how advanced its factories are, and whether it can
package the most complex AI chips fast enough. Each pillar below maps to a line the reader can check on October
15.
(a) HPC revenue: the AI demand line
HPC was 66% of revenue in Q2 2026, about $26.5B (NT$838.5B), up 20% quarter over quarter for two straight
quarters, rising from 57% in Q3 2025 [S1, S2]. In plain terms, two of every three dollars TSMC earned last quarter
came from data center and AI related silicon. That is the clearest real time gauge of how much AI compute is actually
shipping, as opposed to announced.
Smartphone fell to 22% in Q2 2026 from 30% in Q3 2025 [S1]. The mix shift matters more than the total: if HPC
holds or grows its share, the AI buildout is still pulling leading edge capacity. If smartphone or other platforms fill the
gap while HPC stalls, the growth story is broadening but the AI premium in the stock is harder to defend.
(b) Advanced node mix, utilization, and gross margin
Wafer revenue mix in Q2 2026 was 2 nm at 3%, 3 nm at 30%, 5 nm at 33%, and 7 nm at 11%, so advanced nodes
(7 nm and below) were 77% of wafer revenue [S1]. Newer nodes carry higher prices and, once yields mature, higher
margins. Two near term watch items follow from that mix.
3 nm is reportedly set to overtake 5 nm as the top revenue node in the second half of 2026 [S3, Sep 7, 2026].
That crossover would confirm AI and flagship phone demand is migrating to the newest mainstream node.
2 nm entered volume production in Q4 2025, and its ramp dilutes gross margin by 3 to 4 percentage points in the
second half of 2026 [S2]. Early ramps cost money before they make money, because yields start low.
Gross margin reached a record 67.7% in Q2 2026, above the 65.5% to 67.5% guide, helped by cost improvement
and slightly higher utilization [S1]. Q3 2026 gross margin is guided at 65% to 67% [S1]. On utilization, 5 nm and 4
nm are fully booked by AI and HPC demand and 3 nm is heavily utilized [S2]. Full factories spread fixed costs over
more wafers, which supports margin even while 2 nm dilutes it.
(c) CoWoS packaging and capex: the bottleneck and the commitment
CoWoS (Chip on Wafer on Substrate) is the advanced packaging step that joins AI processors to high bandwidth
memory. Without it, a finished AI accelerator cannot ship. CoWoS capacity was about 75,000 to 80,000 wafers per
month at the end of 2025, sold out, targeting about 120,000 to 140,000 wafers per month by late 2026, and
management has acknowledged packaging capacity was limiting customer growth [S2]. Sold out packaging with a
near doubling of capacity is both a constraint today and a revenue runway into 2027.
Capex tells the same story in dollars. At the Q2 2026 call, 2026 capex was raised from $52B to $56B to a range of
$60B to $64B, allocated 70% to 80% to advanced process, 10% to 20% to specialty technologies, and 10% to 20%
to advanced packaging, testing, and mask making [S2]. First half 2026 actual capex was $11.1B in Q1 and $15.7B in
Q2 [S1], so the plan implies a heavier second half. Separately, an additional $100B Arizona investment was
announced on July 16, 2026, bringing the total US commitment to $265B [S2]. Full year 2026 US dollar revenue
growth guidance was raised to slightly above 40%, from close to 30% [S2].

the revenue total, will likely set the day 0 move.

5. Trade structure mechanics for the reaction
Analytical only. This section explains how common structures behave around an earnings print. It is not a recommendation to use any of
them.
Directional long calls and long puts
A long call gains if the share price rises enough, fast enough, to outrun the premium paid and the post earnings drop
in implied volatility (IV crush, below). A long put is the mirror image for a fall. Both are wasting assets into the event:
time decay (theta) accelerates in the final days, and the position needs direction, magnitude, and timing to all line up.
Against a plus or minus 5.0% implied move, a modest move in the right direction can still lose money if it is smaller
than what the premium already priced in.
Long straddles and strangles against the implied move
A long straddle (a call and a put at the same strike) and a long strangle (a call and a put at different strikes) pay for
movement in either direction and do not require picking the sign. Their cost is the combined premium, and their
hurdle is set by the implied move. Using the October 9 figures as illustrative mechanics only: with a $450.19 share
price and an implied move of about plus or minus $22.50 into the October 16 expiry, an at the money straddle priced
near that implied move needs the shares to reach roughly $427.50 on the downside or $472.50 on the upside by
expiry just to cover the move the market already expects, before accounting for the actual premium paid, spreads,
and IV crush. A strangle lowers the upfront premium but pushes those breakeven points further away. These are
arithmetic illustrations of how the structure works, not a suggestion to place the trade.
IV crush, theta, and timing
IV crush: implied volatility is elevated before earnings because the outcome is uncertain. Once the print is out,
that uncertainty collapses and IV typically falls sharply, reducing the value of long options even when the share
price moves favorably. That drop, from pre earnings IV to post earnings IV, is the main cost of holding long
premium through the event.
Theta decay: the October 16 expiry sits one day after the print, so contracts held into the event carry very little
time value buffer. Each day of delay before the print is paid for in decay, and after the print there is almost no time
left for a second move to rescue a position.
Entry timing: entering later means less theta paid before the event, but IV may be different by then, and spreads
can widen. Entering earlier costs more decay but locks the structure sooner. October 9 IV of 36.02% had already
drifted down from intraday prints of 36.66% and 39.92% [S9], a reminder that the price of the structure moves
before the event does.
Exit timing and the fade: with day plus 1 negative in 6 of the last 8 quarters (Section 3), waiting for follow
through has historically worked against holders of long premium, on top of IV crush and theta. That history is why
earnings structures are commonly evaluated on an exit the day of or the day after the print rather than holding
decayed premium into expiry.
Key risks specific to this print
The stock can drift inside plus or minus 5.0% even on a fundamentally good print, leaving long premium
structures short of their breakevens.
The sell the news pattern (6 of 8 day 0 declines) means positive headlines do not guarantee a positive share
reaction.
Pre announced September revenue compresses the headline surprise, so a revenue beat alone may not move
the stock.
Gap risk runs both ways: the realized move can exceed the implied move, as the October 2024 +9.79% day 0
shows.
Earnings expiry contracts can carry wide bid ask spreads and thinner liquidity, raising execution cost exactly
when speed matters.

7. Financing health check: clean
Verdict: clean. Nothing in the reviewed filings suggests the growth program is being funded in a fragile or unusual
way.
The Form 20-F for fiscal 2025 was filed April 16, 2026, and all 2026 Forms 6-K were reviewed. Operating cash
flow in 2025 was NT$2,275B (US$72.5B). Cash plus marketable securities were NT$3,069B (US$97.8B) at the
end of 2025 and grew to NT$3,518B at June 30, 2026, with first half 2026 free cash flow positive in both quarters
[S10, S11].
Bond issuance in 2026 totals only NT$54.1B (about US$1.7B) across three small domestic NT dollar tranches at
1.72% to 2.10%, inside a pre authorized NT$60B program. That is routine top up issuance at about 1.7% of cash
on hand. No 2026 US dollar bond issuance was found, and there are no convertible bonds [S11].
Deloitte and Touche issued an unqualified opinion on the financial statements and internal controls. There has
been no auditor change, no restatement, and no going concern language [S10].
Related party transactions are limited to long standing associates at about 0.9% of revenue, with arm's length
terms stated. There is no buyback program; cash returns run through quarterly dividends, raised to NT$7 per
share for Q1 and Q2 2026 [S11].
The raised $60B to $64B 2026 capex, the $29.4B of capex appropriations approved August 11, 2026, and the
$265B US investment program are funded from operating cash flow and cash reserves, not debt. Aggregate long
term debt is only NT$1,033B (US$32.9B), and operating income covers finance costs about 156.5 times [S11].
On October 8, TSMC disclosed NT$3.83B of intra group lending to TSMC Washington plus parent guarantees to
US and Arizona units. That is ordinary capital support for the US expansion, not a red flag [S11].
