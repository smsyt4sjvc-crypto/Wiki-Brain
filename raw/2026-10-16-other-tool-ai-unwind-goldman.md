# Other tool: "The AI unwind: Putting Goldman's warnings together" — October 16, 2026 (Jake's paste, ~10:05am PT)
(Full text as pasted.)

The central story is that two parts of the AI boom are coming under pressure simultaneously: the leveraged investors holding AI stocks and the credit markets financing AI infrastructure.

Goldman's concern isn't that demand for AI compute has disappeared. It's that compute demand can grow exponentially while the capital available to finance new capacity cannot grow at the same rate.

1. Hedge funds are unwinding the AI trade
Goldman's prime-brokerage data shows that hedge funds reversed all their cumulative net buying of its global AI basket since the beginning of 2025 in just five trading sessions, October 8–14.
Gross leverage 310.3% · Net leverage 73.3% · Gross leverage vs. five-year history 94th percentile · AI net exposure vs. three-year history 83rd percentile.
Phase one was reducing directional exposure. Phase two is reducing the enormous total leverage still on the books. Funds have sold AI longs aggressively, but gross leverage remains near historical extremes. Further deleveraging can require selling additional long positions and closing shorts, regardless of individual companies' fundamentals.
Reuters: global fundamental long/short funds lost 2.9% in the week through October 15, while Asia-focused funds lost 6.8% in the first half of October.

2. AI infrastructure financing is getting more expensive
Goldman/Dealogic: US project-finance bond issuance 2026 exceeds $106 billion vs $37 billion in all of 2025. Total issuance nearly tripled; junk-rated portion ~24% to 35%; risk tied to specific assets, projects, leases and counterparties. The $106B is new issuance, not maturing debt; covers the broader US project-finance market, not exclusively AI. Goldman: stress concentrated in AI-related high-yield credits — neoclouds, frontier-model exposures, data-center projects.

3. CoreWeave
Reuters: ~$50 billion of debt, some bonds yielding 15–16%, CDS ~1,200 bp this week. Sequence: record borrowing → supply up while risk appetite weakens → costs rise, returns compress → marginal expansions delayed/cancelled → expected revenue/earnings fall → more equity selling, credit deterioration, deleveraging. Not yet proof the buildout is contracting.

4. 2027
Goldman projections (as reported in the October 16 credit analysis): ~$300 billion of global project financing in 2027 and ~$420 billion of direct hyperscaler debt issuance. A 3pp increase in financing costs adds $300M/yr interest on a newly financed $10B project (if entirely subject to the increase).

5. Exposure table: CRWV/leveraged neoclouds (refinancing, cash burn, funding expansion) · AI project-finance vehicles (construction completion, tenant credit, contract enforceability) · ORCL (financing costs vs contracted cash flow) · NVDA/AMD/equipment (customer financing constraints could delay orders) · cash-rich hyperscalers (rising capital costs, ROI; stronger financing capacity).

Bottom line: a repricing of weak AI borrowers is manageable; a broad withdrawal of financing would threaten the growth assumptions underlying the whole trade.
