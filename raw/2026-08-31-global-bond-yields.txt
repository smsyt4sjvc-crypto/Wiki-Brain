GLOBAL BOND YIELDS — AUGUST 31, 2026

Yes. This is a genuinely global bond selloff, not just a U.S. Treasury story.

The important qualification is that the exact “highest since” date varies by country and maturity. But across much of the developed world, long-term sovereign borrowing costs are either near multi-year highs or at levels not seen in a decade or more.

THE GLOBAL PICTURE

The easiest way to characterize the current environment is:

JAPAN:
~30-year yield extremes

UNITED KINGDOM:
Borrowing costs at or near multi-decade extremes in portions of the curve

GERMANY:
~15-year extremes

AUSTRALIA:
~15-year extremes

UNITED STATES:
~19-month high in the 10Y, with the 30Y around 5%+

This is therefore not simply:

“Fed rates are high.”

It is increasingly:

“THE GLOBAL COST OF LONG-TERM CAPITAL IS RISING.”

WHY IT IS HAPPENING

Several forces are hitting sovereign bonds simultaneously.

1. STICKY INFLATION

Inflation has not disappeared sufficiently for bond markets to confidently price aggressive monetary easing.

Higher expected inflation means investors demand higher nominal yields to hold long-duration government debt.

2. ENERGY INFLATION

The Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption introduce another inflation impulse through:

• crude oil
• diesel
• LNG
• shipping
• petrochemicals
• transportation
• electricity
• manufacturing inputs

Higher energy prices simultaneously weaken growth and increase inflation.

That is particularly hostile to long-duration bonds.

3. MASSIVE GOVERNMENT BORROWING

Governments are issuing extraordinary amounts of debt to finance:

• deficits
• defense
• infrastructure
• energy security
• industrial policy
• social spending
• interest expense on existing debt

More bond supply requires either:

A) more buyers

or

B) higher yields

to clear the market.

4. FISCAL RISK / TERM PREMIUM

Investors increasingly want more compensation for lending governments money for 10, 20 or 30 years.

That additional compensation is the term premium.

Markets are effectively saying:

“If you want me to lock up capital for decades while deficits remain enormous and inflation uncertainty is elevated, you need to pay me more.”

5. GLOBAL CAPITAL COMPETITION

Governments are no longer borrowing in isolation.

They are competing for capital against an enormous private investment cycle involving:

• AI
• data centers
• semiconductors
• power generation
• transmission
• grid infrastructure
• defense
• manufacturing reshoring
• energy infrastructure

This is important.

AI infrastructure does not need to directly cause inflation for it to influence bond markets.

It creates another massive source of demand for:

CAPITAL
LABOR
POWER
STEEL
COPPER
TRANSFORMERS
TURBINES
SEMICONDUCTORS
CONSTRUCTION

Governments and corporations are effectively bidding against one another for the same finite pool of resources and capital.

6. CENTRAL BANKS HAVE LESS ROOM TO CUT

If inflation remains sticky while energy prices rise, central banks cannot easily respond to weaker growth by aggressively cutting rates.

That removes one of the normal supports for long-duration bonds.

THE UNUSUAL GEOPOLITICAL SIGNAL

Normally when geopolitical risk explodes, investors seek safety in government bonds.

Typical reaction:

GEOPOLITICAL SHOCK
↓
BUY TREASURIES
↓
BOND PRICES RISE
↓
YIELDS FALL

But the current environment is behaving differently.

The geopolitical shock is being interpreted heavily as:

ENERGY SHOCK
+
INFLATION SHOCK
+
FISCAL SHOCK

Therefore:

GEOPOLITICAL ESCALATION
↓
OIL / ENERGY HIGHER
↓
INFLATION EXPECTATIONS HIGHER
↓
CENTRAL BANK CUTS BECOME HARDER
↓
BONDS SELL OFF
↓
YIELDS RISE

That is an important regime signal.

Safe-haven demand is not currently overwhelming inflation and fiscal concerns.

IMPLICATIONS

1. EQUITY VALUATIONS FACE A HIGHER DISCOUNT RATE

The higher the risk-free rate becomes, the less investors should theoretically pay today for earnings expected far in the future.

That particularly affects:

• high-P/E growth stocks
• unprofitable technology
• long-duration equities
• speculative software
• biotech
• companies dependent on repeated refinancing

A 5% Treasury yield creates a much higher hurdle rate for owning risky assets than a 2% Treasury yield.

2. AI STOCKS FACE AN INTERESTING SPLIT

AI demand can remain extremely strong while AI equity valuations become more difficult to sustain.

The infrastructure itself may still experience:

• accelerating GPU demand
• HBM shortages
• networking growth
• power demand
• grid investment
• semiconductor capex

But higher bond yields increase the required return investors demand from those future cash flows.

Therefore:

AI FUNDAMENTALS CAN IMPROVE
WHILE
AI VALUATION MULTIPLES COMPRESS

Both can happen simultaneously.

3. HIGHLY LEVERAGED AI INFRASTRUCTURE BECOMES MORE VULNERABLE

This is especially important given the enormous amount of AI infrastructure increasingly financed through:

• private credit
• asset-backed securities
• structured finance
• equipment financing
• data-center debt
• long-term leases
• project finance

If the risk-free rate rises, all of those financing structures become more expensive.

A project that works economically with:

6% financing

may look considerably worse at:

8–10% financing

especially when utilization assumptions are aggressive.

4. PRIVATE CREDIT GETS MORE DANGEROUS

Higher sovereign yields pull corporate borrowing costs higher.

Credit spreads do not even need to widen dramatically for absolute borrowing costs to become problematic.

Example:

Treasury = 3%
Credit spread = 300 bp
Corporate yield = 6%

versus:

Treasury = 5%
Same 300 bp spread
Corporate yield = 8%

Nothing technically “broke” in the credit spread.

But the company’s refinancing cost increased by 200 basis points.

That matters enormously for leveraged borrowers.

5. REFINANCING RISK RISES

Companies that issued cheap debt between 2020–2022 eventually have to refinance.

Debt issued around:

2%
3%
4%

may roll into:

6%
7%
8%+

That means a larger percentage of corporate cash flow goes toward interest rather than:

• hiring
• capex
• dividends
• buybacks
• acquisitions

6. GOVERNMENTS ENTER A DEBT-INTEREST FEEDBACK LOOP

Higher government borrowing costs increase interest expense.

Higher interest expense increases deficits.

Larger deficits require more bond issuance.

More issuance can push yields higher.

That creates the potential feedback loop:

HIGHER YIELDS
↓
HIGHER GOVERNMENT INTEREST EXPENSE
↓
LARGER DEFICITS
↓
MORE DEBT ISSUANCE
↓
MORE SUPPLY
↓
HIGHER YIELDS

This does not automatically become a sovereign debt crisis.

But it increasingly constrains fiscal policy.

7. CENTRAL BANKS FACE A WORSE TRADEOFF

If economic growth weakens while inflation remains elevated, central banks face:

CUT RATES
→ support growth
→ risk inflation/currency weakness

KEEP RATES HIGH
→ fight inflation
→ increase financial stress

That is much harder than a conventional recession where inflation collapses and central banks can cut aggressively.

8. HOUSING REMAINS PRESSURED

Long-term sovereign yields heavily influence mortgage rates.

If Treasury yields remain elevated:

• mortgage rates remain high
• affordability remains weak
• transaction volumes stay suppressed
• builders face higher financing costs
• commercial real estate refinancing remains difficult

Even if the Fed eventually cuts its overnight rate, mortgage rates do not necessarily collapse if long-duration Treasury yields stay elevated.

9. BANKS FACE MIXED EFFECTS

Higher yields can increase bank lending margins.

But rapid increases in long yields also create:

• unrealized bond losses
• deposit competition
• commercial real estate stress
• borrower defaults
• refinancing problems

The speed of the yield increase matters enormously.

10. THE DOLLAR’S REACTION BECOMES IMPORTANT

Ordinarily higher U.S. yields support the dollar.

But if yields rise because investors demand greater compensation for U.S. fiscal/inflation risk, the relationship becomes more complicated.

A particularly concerning combination would be:

TREASURY YIELDS UP
+
DOLLAR DOWN

That would suggest foreigners are demanding higher yields without simultaneously increasing demand for the currency.

That would be a much more serious fiscal/term-premium signal.

11. GOLD CAN RISE ALONGSIDE YIELDS

Normally high real yields are a headwind for gold.

But gold can still appreciate if investors become concerned about:

• fiscal sustainability
• currency debasement
• geopolitical instability
• sanctions
• reserve diversification
• sovereign creditworthiness

Gold rising simultaneously with long-term sovereign yields would reinforce the idea that this is partly a confidence/fiscal-risk trade rather than simply strong economic growth.

12. COMMODITY PRODUCERS CAN OUTPERFORM

Persistent inflation and geopolitical supply constraints can favor companies tied to:

• oil
• natural gas
• uranium
• copper
• defense materials
• refining
• pipelines
• electrical equipment

particularly when their revenues rise with inflation while existing debt remains fixed-rate.

13. REFINERS MAY HAVE AN ADVANTAGE

If crude/product disruptions push diesel, gasoline and jet-fuel cracks higher, refiners can benefit even while the wider economy suffers from higher energy costs.

This creates another unusual split:

BAD FOR ECONOMY
≠
BAD FOR ENERGY EARNINGS

14. UTILITIES AND POWER INFRASTRUCTURE SPLIT

Traditional regulated utilities can struggle when Treasury yields rise because investors compare their dividend yields against safer government bonds.

But companies directly exposed to:

• data-center power
• nuclear
• gas generation
• transformers
• transmission
• grid equipment

can experience sufficiently strong earnings growth to offset some valuation pressure.

15. SMALL CAPS ARE PARTICULARLY EXPOSED

Small companies generally:

• carry more floating-rate debt
• refinance more frequently
• have weaker balance sheets
• have less access to cheap capital

Therefore prolonged high yields tend to favor large profitable companies with strong balance sheets over heavily leveraged small caps.

16. TREASURY MARKET VOLATILITY MATTERS BEYOND TREASURIES

Treasuries are the foundation of global collateral markets.

Higher Treasury volatility affects:

• repo
• derivatives
• hedge funds
• mortgage-backed securities
• bank balance sheets
• foreign reserve managers
• insurance portfolios
• pension funds

The danger is not simply that yields reach 5%.

The danger is that yields reach those levels extremely quickly.

17. JAPAN MAY BE ONE OF THE MOST IMPORTANT MARKETS TO WATCH

Japanese yields near multi-decade highs matter globally because Japan has historically supplied enormous amounts of cheap capital to the rest of the world.

If Japanese investors can suddenly earn attractive yields domestically, they have less incentive to own:

• U.S. Treasuries
• European sovereign bonds
• foreign corporate bonds

Capital returning to Japan could remove an important marginal buyer from Western bond markets.

18. GLOBAL SYNCHRONIZATION MAKES THIS MORE IMPORTANT

One country’s yields rising can often reflect local fiscal or political problems.

But when yields rise simultaneously across:

U.S.
UK
Germany
Japan
Australia
France
Italy
Canada

the signal is different.

It suggests a broader repricing of:

INFLATION
+
FISCAL RISK
+
TERM PREMIUM
+
CAPITAL SCARCITY

rather than an isolated national event.

THE MOST IMPORTANT THING TO WATCH

The danger level rises considerably if we begin seeing several of these together:

• U.S. 10Y decisively above 5%
• U.S. 30Y continuing materially above 5%
• German Bund yields making fresh cycle highs
• Japanese yields continuing to break multi-decade highs
• UK gilt yields accelerating higher
• corporate credit spreads widening simultaneously
• Treasury auction tails worsening
• foreign demand at Treasury auctions weakening
• dollar falling despite higher U.S. yields
• gold rising alongside yields
• equities falling as yields rise
• MOVE Index / bond volatility accelerating
• mortgage rates moving materially higher
• investment-grade and high-yield refinancing costs jumping

That would indicate the market is moving beyond:

“HIGHER FOR LONGER”

and toward:

“THE PRICE OF GLOBAL CAPITAL IS BEING STRUCTURALLY REPRICED HIGHER.”

BOTTOM LINE

Yes — globally, sovereign yields are extraordinarily elevated.

Not every country’s 10-year bond is literally at a multi-year record on the same day, but the synchronization is unusual:

Japan is near 30-year yield extremes.
Germany and Australia are around 15-year extremes.
The U.K. is experiencing multi-decade borrowing-cost pressure.
The U.S. 10Y is near 4.8% and the 30Y is around 5%+.

The biggest implication is that this isn’t merely tighter monetary policy anymore.

The bond market is increasingly pricing a world with:

MORE DEBT
+
MORE GOVERNMENT SPENDING
+
MORE DEFENSE SPENDING
+
MORE AI/INFRASTRUCTURE CAPEX
+
MORE ENERGY CONSTRAINTS
+
STICKIER INFLATION
+
LESS CENTRAL-BANK FLEXIBILITY

There is an enormous amount of capital competing for a finite pool of savings and real resources.

If that persists, the structurally important market variable may no longer be simply where the Fed sets overnight rates.

It may be how high the global economy must raise LONG-TERM REAL RETURNS to attract enough capital to finance everything being demanded of it.
