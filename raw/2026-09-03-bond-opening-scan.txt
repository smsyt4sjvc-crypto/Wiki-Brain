The opening rally is directly visible in the liquid Treasury benchmarks: shortly after Waller’s remarks, the 2-year was around 4.32%, 10-year around 4.74%, and 30-year around 5.23%, versus Wednesday’s official close of roughly 4.39%, 4.79% and 5.27%. Initial claims rose only slightly to 206,000, while Waller indicated he is leaning against a September hike absent hotter inflation data.  Treasury’s official Sept. 2 curve gives the prior-close anchors used for the intermediate maturities; because no single public feed supplies every maturity at exactly 9:30 ET, those intermediate opening points are intentionally rounded rather than presented as fake precision. 

The latest official real curve has 5Y at 2.19%, 10Y at 2.45% and 30Y at 2.98% on Sept. 2. The ~2.35% and ~2.34% 5Y/10Y breakevens in the report are simple same-date nominal-minus-real par-curve calculations, not separate intraday market quotes. 

The new development worth emphasizing is CoreWeave. Five-year CDS is now trading above 800 bp, which MarketWatch translates to roughly a 40%-45% five-year default probability under its assumptions—dramatically worse than broad IG/HY spread behavior.  CoreWeave’s own 10-Q reports $35.6B of indebtedness and $558M of Q2 interest expense, including 9.75%, 9.625% and 8.50% senior notes and large floating-rate DDTL facilities. 

The accounting side is equally relevant: CoreWeave says it reassessed a data-center service provider as a VIE during Q2 because the adequacy of the provider’s equity at risk changed, reran the primary-beneficiary test, and still did not consolidate it. Its remaining completion-funding commitment has no contractual cap. Separately, commitments to two other unconsolidated development VIEs reached $1.7B, including $500M added during Q2. 

Meta’s latest filing confirms the huge commitment acceleration: $278.99B of uncommenced leases versus $182.88B in Q1, another ~$68B signed in July, and $349.31B of contractual commitments versus $237.67B in Q1.  SpaceX meanwhile reports $13.406B of other financings, including failed-sale-leaseback AI infrastructure debt, $39.512B of total debt plus finance leases, and confirms that its $20B bridge refinanced legacy X/xAI debt before the bridge itself was replaced with $25B of SpaceX notes.  Tesla’s Q2 filing still limits the visible direct contagion channel to its $2B SpaceX investment and $405M of H1 SpaceX Megapack revenue, with other related-party transactions described as immaterial. 

The sovereign/private-capital collision remains intact. August U.S. investment-grade issuance reached a record $164B, driven heavily by AI financing, and BofA expects roughly $190B in September.  At the same time, the federal deficit reached $432B in July and $1.799T for FY2026 through July, already exceeding the entire FY2025 deficit with two months left.
