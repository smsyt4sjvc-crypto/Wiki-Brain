2. AMD / FERMI: NEW CREDIT SUPPORT IS A MATERIAL SHADOW-LEVERAGE DEVELOPMENT

Project Matador conceptual rendering—not completed operating capacity.

Confirmed company announcement — October 13

Fermi America announced that it signed a credit-support agreement with AMD related to the first phase of Project Matador in Texas.

The supported lease covers:

222 MW of gross power capacity.

The companies also signed a framework for future development of up to:

650 MW.

Fermi describes the credit agreement as a milestone toward obtaining project financing.

The first tenant arrangement involves AI cloud provider TensorWave.

The previously announced tenant lease has been described as potentially generating approximately $6.5B over 15 years.

Fermi shares surged following today’s announcement. 

Shadow-leverage classification

LEVEL 2 — NEW CREDIT SUPPORT

This fits the framework’s category of newly announced credit-support/backstop arrangements.

However, an important distinction remains:

We have confirmation of the agreement, but not sufficient public detail to quantify AMD’s maximum exposure to loss.

We also do not yet have enough information to conclude that AMD has provided an unconditional guarantee of every TensorWave lease obligation.

The company’s announcement specifically identifies conditions affecting AMD’s obligations and the lease closing.

Project financing is not yet finalized.

Why it matters

This is precisely how AI infrastructure risk can migrate between companies.

A developer may have the land, power equipment and prospective customer.

But lenders still need confidence that the tenant will pay.

A stronger technology company’s credit support can make financing possible before the data center becomes operational.

That is economically valuable—but it creates contingent exposure somewhere else.

What’s uncertain

The major unanswered questions are:

* Exact AMD guarantee or support amount.
* Termination and performance conditions.
* Whether credit support covers rent, construction obligations or other liabilities.
* Whether AMD receives control rights.
* Whether project lenders gain recourse against AMD.
* Timing of actual energized and revenue-producing capacity.

Affected: AMD, FRMI, private-credit lenders, AI cloud operators and competing data-center developers.

Trade implication: strategically positive for AMD accelerator deployment and Fermi’s financing prospects.

But AMD’s contingent exposure needs to be evaluated alongside GPU sales revenue.

The next document to examine is the actual agreement—not the headline MW number.

3. SEMICONDUCTORS: CHINA INTRODUCES ANOTHER POTENTIAL SOURCE OF MEMORY SUPPLY

Illustrative semiconductor manufacturing image.

Confirmed market developments

Semiconductor shares sold off across Asia and the United States.

South Korea’s Kospi declined more than 6%.

Samsung: approximately -7.4%.

SK hynix: approximately -10%.

Micron: approximately -5%.

Chinese memory manufacturer CXMT announced progress on a new DRAM architecture intended to improve memory density.

Broadcom CEO Hock Tan also expressed concern that some AI investors are extrapolating experimental customer revenue into overly optimistic long-term financial assumptions. 

Why this directly matters to our volume thesis

Your argument has two parts:

First: semiconductor revenue growth is increasingly driven by price and mix rather than equivalent physical-volume growth.

Second: expanding usable capacity can eventually release deferred supply quickly enough to collapse shortage premiums.

Today’s CXMT development is relevant to the second part.

A new viable competitor can change future supply expectations before meaningful additional chips are shipped.

But today’s announcement is not proof that qualified memory supply has already increased.

What remains uncertain

CXMT’s actual:

Commercial shipment volumes.

Production yields.

Customer qualifications.

Capacity utilization.

Competitive pricing.

Without those, we cannot determine how quickly its new products will influence global DRAM supply.

The distinction matters because technology announcements are frequently months—or years—ahead of large-scale usable production.

Market impact

Negative for memory suppliers if investors begin discounting future ASP declines.

Potentially positive for large memory customers if supply competition reduces prices.

Affected: MU, Samsung, SK hynix, NVDA, AMD, AVGO, equipment suppliers and HBM-related companies.

Trade implication

The immediate risk is valuation compression before reported revenue starts declining.

The next major physical-volume checkpoint remains:

Thursday, October 15: TSMC Q3 results.

We already know Q3 revenue accelerated sharply.

We need the corresponding 12-inch-equivalent wafer shipments.

If wafer growth remains modest while revenue rises dramatically, the price/mix divergence becomes more apparent.

For memory, watch ASP changes and qualified bit supply—not announcements about future capacity alone.
