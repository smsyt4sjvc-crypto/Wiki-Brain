# Axios — "Fed rate dissenters make their case for higher rates" (Courtenay Brown)
Pasted by Jake 2026-07-31 ~8:50am PT. Direct quotes from the three dissent statements —
the most primary Fed source this vault has on the 7/29 meeting.

DISSENTERS: Kashkari (Minneapolis), Hammack (Cleveland), Logan (Dallas).
All three voted for a QUARTER-POINT INCREASE at the 7/29 meeting (majority held 9-3).

KASHKARI (statement Friday morning):
  "I increasingly believe that monetary policy does have an important role to play in
   addressing a series of successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher
   inflation."
  - Says the Fed should USUALLY look through temporary supply shocks, BUT after years of
    disruption (pandemic, Ukraine, tariffs, Middle East conflict) inflation risks becoming
    ENTRENCHED. Invoked a parallel to THE 1970s.
  - Pointed to one ADDITIONAL factor: the "massive investment in DATA CENTERS," which he
    said has added a NEW DEMAND ELEMENT to the inflation outlook.
  - "[A] potential series of small policy moves would be better than waiting and eventually
    concluding that even bolder actions were necessary."

HAMMACK (separate statement):
  - "not confident" inflation will return to the 2% target on its own.
  - Seeing "inflationary pressures coming from the DEMAND side of the economy, as well."
  - Businesses across the Cleveland Fed district report pricing pressures are BROADENING
    rather than fading; consumers "expressing DESPAIR over persistently higher prices."
  - "A higher federal funds rate would help restrain economic activity and reduce
    inflationary pressures."

LOGAN (statement):
  - "Labor, consumption and financial market conditions indicate that monetary policy is
    NOT RESTRAINING the economy."
  - "Without any policy restraint, inflation will likely continue to trend above target
    until there's an unanticipated shock."
  - "Modest action in the near term would reduce the likelihood of needing to take sharper
    action later."

AXIOS FRAMING: the dissents "lay out a blueprint for the Fed's hawkish wing." The open
question is "whether that argument persuades more policymakers... in the weeks ahead."
Explicitly NOT a call for an aggressive tightening campaign -- small moves now while the
labor market is healthy, to avoid larger moves later.
