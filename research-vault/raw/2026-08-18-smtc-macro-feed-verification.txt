August 18, 2026 | Complete 75-item feed ledger
Bottom line: The major Iran/oil/yield/inflation posts were broadly real. Several headlines layered editorial causality or overstatement onto the underlying facts. Nothing in the feed identified a new Semtech-specific fundamental break.

Executive Summary
The feed supported the macro mechanism behind the semiconductor selloff: stalled Iran talks and renewed regional risk kept oil and refined-product inflation pressure elevated; Treasury yields rose sharply; high-duration AI and semiconductor stocks were hit hardest.
The strongest hard-data chain was: stalled Iran negotiations -> UAE missile alert and continuing Hormuz disruption -> diesel near $5.45/gal and record diesel refining margins -> higher inflation/term-premium fears -> 10-year Treasury near 4.75% and 30-year near 5.31% -> pressure on growth/semiconductor valuations.
There were meaningful counter-signals: GDPNow remained very strong at 4.0%, import/export prices fell, and Home Depot did not collapse. The tape looked more like an energy-driven inflation scare, long-end bond repricing, geopolitical stagnation and profit-taking in extended semiconductor/AI trades than a sudden collapse in the real economy.
For SMTC, the feed contained no new customer loss, 1.6T problem, HieFo issue, order cancellation, capacity problem or earnings warning. That supported viewing the sharp SMTC decline as sector/macro liquidation rather than direct evidence that the Aug. 25 earnings thesis had changed.

5. What Mattered for SMTC and Semiconductors
The feed confirmed the macro mechanism identified during the trading session. The combination of stalled Iran negotiations, renewed regional security alerts, Hormuz disruption, elevated diesel/refining margins and sharply higher long-end Treasury yields created a hostile backdrop for high-duration semiconductor and AI names.
At the same time, the feed did not support a "the economy suddenly broke" interpretation. GDPNow remained 4.0%, import/export prices were soft, and Home Depot reported continued positive comps. The evidence was more consistent with an energy-driven inflation scare, term-premium repricing and sector de-risking/profit-taking.
Most important for the SMTC earnings trade: there was no new Semtech-specific fundamental negative in the supplied feed - no customer loss, 1.6T demand problem, HieFo issue, backlog/order cancellation, capacity/capex problem, China-optics warning or earnings preannouncement. That supports treating the day's SMTC collapse as part of the sector/macro liquidation unless new company-specific evidence emerges.
