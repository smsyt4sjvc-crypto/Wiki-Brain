4. What the scanner is actually saying
The strongest cross-asset message is sector compression, not generalized liquidation. Semiconductors and memory are being repriced much harder than broad equities. The lower VIX and positive small-cap tape in the supplied snapshot argue against labeling the move as a market-wide panic.
Memory is simultaneously strong fundamentally and fragile financially. Record profits, foundry price hikes and an enormous SK Hynix buyback support the “tight supply / cash generation” side of the thesis. The size of the equity drawdown, Q2 expectation miss and violent Korean leverage dynamics support the “expectations and positioning were extreme” side.
AI capex remains real, but financing quality matters more now. The market is no longer rewarding project size by itself. Tenor, spread, syndication take-up, lease backstops and who ultimately carries residual-value risk are increasingly the variables that distinguish durable capex from fragile financial engineering.
Physical energy routes remain an underpriced tail channel. Hormuz transit is visibly constrained and CPC has not demonstrated a stable post-attack operating regime. Flat-to-modestly higher crude prices do not eliminate the route-risk signal; they may instead reflect offsets from demand expectations, inventories or alternative flows.
Bottom line: The scanner is useful and directionally accurate. The highest-value corrections are to stop treating the $130B Microsoft lease figure as additive, downgrade the Senate/CXMT item from “bill” risk to “letter + existing procurement wall,” treat CPC as impaired-but-not-closed, and normalize the Micron CEO sale as a pre-planned 10b5-1 transaction rather than standalone bearish evidence.
