Iran/Hormuz two-week digest, pasted by Jake 2026-08-13 ~9:00am PDT. COMPILED DIGEST, not primary.
Iran insists Hormuz stays closed/tightly controlled until the US: permanently ends the war, withdraws
forces from the region, lifts the naval blockade on Iranian ports, removes all sanctions, releases frozen
assets, pays reparations for war damage, ceases threats, and in some statements ends conflicts involving
Hezbollah, Hamas, the Houthis. Successive SNSC secretaries: no vessel transits safely without Iranian
authorization; Oman route talks do not equal reopening. US (Trump): demands immediate complete opening of
all Hormuz lanes WITHOUT TOLLS, end to attacks on commercial shipping, resolution of the nuclear program;
claims "total control" via a naval blockade that redirected dozens of vessels, disabled several including
the Panama-flagged M/V Vela Nova (Hellfire missiles from an MH-60 after it ignored orders), boarded others.
Attacks both directions: Iran/proxies struck commercial tankers and cargo in Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb
(Egyptian-owned Tihamah, multiple crew dead; earlier Qatari, Saudi hulls), missiles/drones at US facilities
in Kuwait and Bahrain, Kurdish groups in Iraq. US struck Iranian air defenses, coastal radars, anti-ship
missiles, IRGC boats. Shipping through Hormuz far below pre-war norms, single-digit daily transits reported
in recent days, oil elevated. State Dept directed Middle East embassies to prepare contingency plans for
prolonged operations with minimal essential staff, after ordered departures from Bahrain, Iraq, Jordan,
Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE and temporary suspension in Kuwait.
TWO-WEEK READ (through Aug 13 2026): Iran is converting Hormuz from a wartime weapon into negotiating
leverage. Iran and Oman made substantial progress on a shipping arrangement that could give Tehran
authority over inbound Gulf traffic, then Iran hardened: no full reopening unless Washington lifts
sanctions/blockade, releases frozen assets, provides security guarantees, addresses war compensation.
US-Iran talks deadlocked; an Iranian source told Reuters there has been "absolutely no progress" on
reviving the June interim agreement. Oil jumped ~5% on August 10 as optimism over a quick Hormuz
settlement faded. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei replaced Iran's top security official with Mohsen
Rezaei, veteran former IRGC commander, concentrating security decisions around experienced hardliners.
Nuclear unresolved; bargaining center shifted to Hormuz, sanctions relief, frozen assets, regional
security structure. Read: Iran's position improved over two weeks - no final deal, but Washington and the
Gulf states are now negotiating around an Iranian role in managing the chokepoint. Danger: the stronger
bargaining position raises the odds of miscalculation if Washington refuses.
