butane/propane. • The vessel declares an Iraqi port as destination — a routing pattern consistent with masking Iranian-origin cargo, since OFAC i [OFAC] -designated vessels are barred from moving Iranian energy commodities. • The vessel held an identical course and speed for 74 consecutive minutes on 31 July with zero variation, a signature consistent with active AIS manipulation. ▸ MIOC assessment: the spoofing signature indicates a deliberate cargo-masking transit rather than an AIS malfunction. 🌊 Hormuz Transit Volume Stays Near Standstill • Only 5 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz in the 24-hour window ending 21:00 UTC 31 July (4 outbound, 1 inbound) — a fraction of the roughly 140 vessels/day pre-crisis baseline. • Three of the five crossings carry OFAC i [OFAC] SDN designations: the inbound Iran-flagged container vessel and two outbound tankers, one with dark-fleet and false-flag designations. • The dominant cargo was a 2.14M-bbl crude lift by a VLCC-class tanker loaded at Al Basrah, Iraq, transited fully AIS-transmitting. ▸ MIOC assessment: the strait remains disrupted, with sanctioned tonnage accounting for the majority of an already-thin transit volume. 🛰️ EO Imagery Confirms Recurring Dark-Vessel Transits • MIOC satellite (EO) imagery analysis has independently identified roughly a dozen distinct dark vessels — commercial hulls broadcasting no AIS — transiting the Strait of Hormuz in both directions since the current crisis began. • The identified hulls range from small cargo/bulk carriers up to VLCC-class tankers, spanning the full size spectrum of commercial shipping rather than a single vessel class or operator. ▸ MIOC assessment: an ongoing pattern of shadow-fleet / Iran-linked shipping using EO-only, no-AIS transits to bypass AIS-based monitoring during strait passage. 🚢 Bulk Carrier Reverses Off Southern Corridor Approach • A Liberia-flagged bulk carrier was observed turning around on 31 July at 09:21 UTC after approaching the strait's southern corridor. • The reversal followed an IRGC warning issued to the vessel over VHF radio. • The vessel's declared destination has not changed following the reversal, with no updated routing broadcast as of this reporting. ▸ MIOC assessment: consistent with the broader pattern of vessels declining to commit to the southern corridor amid elevated risk, reinforced here by direct IRGC intervention.
