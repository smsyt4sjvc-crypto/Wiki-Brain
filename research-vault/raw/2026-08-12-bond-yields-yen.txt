6. Bond-yen relationship
Higher U.S. short-term yields generally increase the carry advantage of holding dollars over yen and tend to support USD/JPY.
Falling 2-year Treasury yields reduce the expected policy-rate advantage of the dollar and can support the yen.
A high or rising U.S. 10-year/30-year yield can still support dollar assets, but at extreme levels it may also increase financial-stability and intervention concerns.
If USD/JPY pushes through 160 while U.S. yields remain elevated, the probability of renewed intervention or stronger BOJ signaling rises.
A sharp yen rally can trigger carry-trade unwinds, forcing leveraged investors to reduce positions in other risk assets and potentially creating cross-market volatility.

8. Bottom line
Bonds: The CPI report eased pressure at the front end, but long yields remain high. The curve is positively sloped from 2 years through 30 years, with roughly +48 bp from 2s10s and +104 bp from 2s30s using representative post-CPI levels.
Yen: USD/JPY near 159.3 is still a weak-yen regime and uncomfortably close to 160. The rate differential still favors the dollar, but intervention risk is now a major constraint on chasing USD/JPY higher.
Positioning: CFTC non-commercial traders are still net short yen, but the position has been slashed from about -163,000 to -45,000 contracts. That makes the trade less crowded, while also showing how quickly official intervention can force a reversal.
