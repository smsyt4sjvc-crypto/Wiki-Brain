How to read this report
Evidence hierarchy
FILED means a regulatory filing or filed exhibit. ANNOUNCED means a company or transaction-party statement. REPORTED means attributable press reporting where contract documents or full books are not public. DERIVED means arithmetic calculated from cited figures. No undisclosed hedge, swap, margin agreement, liquidation preference, side letter or private-company ownership percentage is treated as known.

The field is mapped as a chain rather than a list of companies: operating company → customer contract or lease → special-purpose vehicle or joint venture → lender/bond buyer → alternative manager, insurer, pension, bank warehouse line or wealth vehicle. The same project can appear in several accounting categories. A lease commitment can support SPV debt; a guarantee can protect the debt; and a cloud-purchase commitment can be the revenue source for the lessee. Those figures cannot be added without tracing overlap.
“Off balance sheet” is used narrowly: a legal obligation, asset or financing vehicle not yet recognized as conventional debt on the operating company’s current balance sheet. It does not mean secret or fraudulent. Many items are disclosed in footnotes as uncommenced leases, purchase commitments, guarantees, residual-value support, credit derivatives, variable-interest entities or customer prepayments.

What is moving now
Bending Spoons is buying Airtable for approximately $1.285B enterprise value and $2.25B implied equity value, using Airtable’s roughly $965M of net cash as the bridge between the two values. That crystallizes a severe late-2021 SaaS mark-down while preserving the fact that shareholders receive the cash on the balance sheet. [S1,S2,S3]
Situational Awareness transferred its public-equity portfolio to Citadel after reported losses and financing pressure. Its March 31 13F showed $13.677B of reportable value, dominated by put positions; press reports put total AUM around or above $20B. Private assets and non-13F positions are not disclosed. [S5,S6,S7,S8]
Private-credit wealth vehicles are not paying all requested withdrawals. Q2 requests reached $15.6B across widely held funds, while $5.9B was paid. The funds are operating according to contractual 5% quarterly repurchase limits, but the scale of unmet requests demonstrates the liquidity mismatch. [S31,S32,S33,S34,S35]
The hyperscaler build is migrating from direct capex into combinations of uncommenced leases, customer commitments, guarantees, residual-value support, SPVs and private placements. The economic risk remains connected to the operating companies even when legal debt sits in a project vehicle. [S10,S11,S12,S13,S14,S16,S25]
Legacy leveraged software is repricing at the same time that managers are extending longer-duration capital to AI infrastructure. Medallia’s lenders became owners; Proofpoint’s refinancing cost reportedly rose toward 9.3% from roughly 3%; Blue Owl is shifting its growth emphasis from direct lending toward real assets. [S36,S38,S39]

Primary conclusion
The present system is not simply “Big Tech borrowing.” It is a layered transfer of duration and utilization risk. Hyperscalers retain customer, lease and guarantee exposure; SPVs issue debt; private-credit and fixed-income managers hold the paper; wealth vehicles offer limited quarterly liquidity; and legacy software loans are being restructured while new AI real-asset loans are originated.

Right: the operating business is valued at only about $1.285B, roughly 88% below the 2021 pre-money valuation and slightly below the $1.36B of total capital raised. That is a severe economic reset. [S1,S2,S3]
Wrong if stated literally: Airtable did not sell for only $1.285B. That is enterprise value. Shareholders receive an implied $2.25B equity value because the approximately $965M of cash belongs to the company and therefore to its shareholders. [S2,S3]
Not publicly calculable: Benchmark, Coatue, D1, Thrive, Greenoaks and XN returns. Exact ownership, entry cost, dilution, secondaries, liquidation preferences, participation rights and internal marks are private. Early investors can still earn positive returns even while the late round is deeply impaired. [S1]

Critical 13F limitation

Vehicle-specific signals
BCRED disclosed a 96.1 overall portfolio mark as of April 30, 2026, while the bottom 5% of its private-debt portfolio was marked at 68.3. That dispersion matters more than the headline NAV: the bulk of the book can remain near par while a small set of sponsor-backed loans absorbs most impairment. BCRED’s Class I shares had generated a 9.3% annualized total return since inception and paid a 10.0% distribution rate, numbers that should be compared with the fund’s nonaccruals, PIK income and realized losses rather than treated as cash yield alone. [S32]
OTIC received $1.1B of Q2 tender demand, equal to 38.1% of March 31 shares. It offered $141.8M, equal to 5%, and said that represented roughly 13% of shares tendered. The fund disclosed $1.3B of liquidity and 0.78x net debt-to-equity, below its stated 0.90x–1.25x target range. [S34,S46]
OCIC’s Q2 offer was approximately $957.8M, equal to 5% of outstanding shares at March 31. In June it separately issued $500M of 6.550% notes due 2031. Debt issuance can support asset growth and liquidity, but it also makes the BDC’s own liability stack relevant when underlying borrowers are refinancing at higher coupons. [S35,S37]
Apollo Debt Solutions received requests equal to 16.8% of shares, honored 5%, estimated $0.7B of gross outflows, received $0.3B of subscriptions and expected a $0.4B net outflow. Onshore requests were 4.3%; offshore requests were 12.5%. [S33]
What a gate means
A 5% repurchase limit is a contractual design feature, not itself a default. The stress signal is persistent oversubscription: investors cannot all leave at stated NAV, so the manager must preserve liquidity, slow originations, borrow, sell liquid assets or accept a longer redemption queue. If marks later fall, investors waiting in the queue bear that change.

Blue Owl’s strategic pivot
Blue Owl’s direct-lending assets fell to about 35% of total assets from nearly 50% two years earlier, while real assets grew from $12B in 2021 to $89.4B at June 2026. This is not a retreat from private markets; it is a shift from sponsor-backed corporate loans toward data centers, infrastructure and long-duration real assets. [S36]
Benefit: real-asset projects can be supported by investment-grade leases, hard assets and contracted payments. [S11,S36]
Risk transfer: technology-utilization and residual-value risk moves into project debt, lease structures and guarantees rather than disappearing. [S10,S11]
Liquidity conflict: wealth vehicles can face redemption pressure at the same time the manager commits capital to 15–30-year infrastructure. [S34,S35,S36]

Why the labels matter
VC funds are generally closed-end vehicles with long holding periods. They can carry private marks for years and distribute proceeds only when an exit occurs. Airtable converts an internal mark into a transaction price.
Crossover funds can own private rounds and public securities. Their 13F reveals only reportable public longs and options, not the private investment, short hedge or financing that may offset it.
Private-credit managers can originate directly, hold loans in BDCs, sell pieces to insurers, finance assets through SPVs and manage project debt. The manager name does not identify the ultimate risk holder; the holding vehicle does.
A hedge fund can use gross exposure and financing far above NAV. Situational Awareness’s 13F is therefore a position inventory, not a complete balance sheet.

10.2 Accounting definitions
Term
Definition
Enterprise value (EV)
Equity value plus debt and preferred claims minus cash; approximates value of operations independent of cash financing.
Equity value
Value attributable to shareholders after debt-like claims; includes cash owned by the company.
Uncommenced lease
Signed lease whose start date has not occurred; generally absent from current lease liability until commencement but disclosed in footnotes.
Finance lease
Lease economically similar to asset financing; asset and liability recognized, with interest and amortization components.
Operating lease
Lease recognized as right-of-use asset/liability under current GAAP, usually with single lease expense.
Residual-value guarantee
Promise to cover a shortfall in asset/project value under specified termination or nonrenewal conditions.
Credit derivative / backstop
Contract whose maximum notional exposure can exceed expected loss; fair value and trigger terms determine actual balance-sheet effect.
RPO / backlog
Contracted future revenue not yet recognized. It is not cash and can include long-duration or cancellable/conditional elements depending on contract.
PIK interest
Interest added to loan principal rather than paid in cash; increases reported income and claim size but not immediate liquidity.
Nontraded BDC
Closed-end lending vehicle sold through wealth channels; shares are not exchange traded and repurchases are limited/discretionary.
13F reported value
Quarter-end reportable long securities and listed options. It excludes most shorts, swaps, financing and private assets.

Closing synthesis
The same capital is now moving through three distinct repricings. First, 2021 venture equity is being realized at lower values, as Airtable demonstrates. Second, leveraged software debt is refinancing at materially higher coupons or converting into creditor ownership, as Proofpoint and Medallia demonstrate. Third, alternative managers are redeploying capital into AI infrastructure through leases, SPVs, guarantees and project debt whose duration is often longer than the customer contracts supporting it.
The practical surveillance framework is therefore not “which company has the most debt.” It is: who signs the customer contract; who owns the asset; who issues the debt; who guarantees payment or residual value; who holds the paper; what vehicle promises liquidity; and what happens when the customer contract expires before the asset or lease obligation.
