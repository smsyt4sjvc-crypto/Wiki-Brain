This is the first entry in a series working through the neocloud sector: Nebius, CoreWeave, IREN, Nscale, Lambda, and others, that exist largely to buy Nvidia GPUs, build data centers around them, and rent the resulting compute to hyperscalers and AI labs.

Since companies first began becoming neoclouds, I have been pointing out that Jensen found an opportunity to diversify his customer base beyond the very few hyperscalers, and a genius way to generate demand for Nvidia by adopting them and making them Nvidia's own SPVs. In an April 2026 interview with Dwarkesh Patel, Jensen Huang acknowledged my thesis and said it out loud: "In the case of clouds, if we didn't support CoreWeave to exist, these neoclouds, these AI clouds, wouldn't exist. If we didn't help CoreWeave exist, they would not exist. If we didn't support Nscale, they wouldn't be where they are today. If we didn't support Nebius, they wouldn't be what they are today." And, confirming that Nvidia wants lots of neoclouds, not just one: "Don't pick winners. Either let them all take care of themselves, or take care of all of them."

That is, in Nvidia's own CEO's words, the operating logic behind this sector. Rather than build a cloud business on its own balance sheet, which would put Nvidia's own results directly at risk if utilization or pricing disappointed, Nvidia has helped create and sustain an entire class of nominally independent companies that take on the capital outlay, the construction risk, and the debt of building AI data centers, while Nvidia supplies the chips, frequently invests equity alongside the debt, and in a growing number of cases finances the build out directly. If a given neocloud stumbles, Nvidia writes off an investment. If it succeeds, Nvidia has sold it tens of billions of dollars of GPUs along the way. Either outcome serves the same underlying purpose: sustaining the revenue growth that a company now valued in the trillions of dollars needs to keep justifying its multiple, at a scale where organic hyperscaler demand alone is no longer enough to fill the gap every quarter. In substance, each neocloud functions as a special purpose vehicle for Nvidia's own demand, independently capitalized, independently indebted, and independently exposed if the underlying economics do not hold, while Nvidia's own balance sheet stays comparatively clean.

[NVDA] for the first time changed its revenue reporting by splitting Data Center revenue into two customer market platforms: Hyperscale and AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise (ACIE)... That means Jensen is very serious about making the neoclouds a leading driver of Nvidia's Data Center revenue growth going forward. To do so, the support Nvidia provides to these SPVs has increased from hundreds of millions to billions, in many forms, using all the creativity that financing and accounting allow, including gray areas.

Five structural observations: (1) mostly/exclusively Nvidia racks; (2) lease facilities + bring hardware, team with legacy DC builders, some via joint project SPVs ("why carry that debt directly if it can sit outside?"); (3) many are crypto veterans (licenses, legacy DCs, GPU experience); (4) most have 1-3 big customers — a hyperscaler renting onward to the same AI lab, or another neocloud renting it out again: "many are renting to each other"; (5) big revenue promises / fast deployment claims, while many deals are easily terminable, many are MOUs, and funding to complete is a question.

Series question, per name, starting with Nebius: real path to survival and profitability, or structurally dependent on continuous debt, dilution, and Nvidia's own support?
