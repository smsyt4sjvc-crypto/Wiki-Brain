Optimus Supply-Chain, Magnets & Physical-AI Investability Map
Research compilation from the working thread • August 22, 2026
Core conclusion
The highest-value Optimus supply-chain exposure sits in precision motion, compute/electronics, hands/interconnects, batteries, and—strategically—rare-earth permanent magnets. The magnet thesis is not that humanoids already consume enough NdFeB to move the global market. The thesis is that Western governments and OEMs are reserving, financing and qualifying non-China mine-to-magnet capacity before physical-AI volume arrives.
Critical correction: the widely circulated Sanhua RMB 5 billion / ~$685 million / ~180,000-robot Tesla actuator order was explicitly denied by Sanhua and must not be treated as a confirmed purchase order. Chinese supplier capacity planning is evidence of preparation, not a binding Tesla volume commitment.
Scope note. This document consolidates the substantive Optimus, memory-cycle analogue, magnets/rare-earths, China supply-chain, government-funding and supplier-map discussion from the working thread. It preserves the evidence grades and known contradictions rather than converting secondary reports into confirmed facts.

1. Executive synthesis
Treating “consumable” broadly as anything consumed once per robot plus parts likely to wear or require replacement, the most attractive Optimus supply-chain categories are:
Precision motion: roller screws, ball screws, harmonic/cycloidal/planetary reducers, motors, integrated actuators, bearings, encoders and force/torque sensing.
Compute and electronics: memory, Tesla-designed AI boards, power electronics, PCBAs, flexible printed circuits and connectors.
Hands and interconnects: compact actuators, tactile/force sensing, tendons, bearings and dynamic wiring.
Batteries and power distribution.
Structures and castings.
Factory-process consumption: adhesives, precision fastening, inspection and calibration equipment.
The precision-motion stack has the best combination of high content per robot, difficult manufacturing, potential wear and constrained capacity. The strongest confirmed Tesla supplier evidence is currently in memory and semiconductors. Direct mechanical names have greater Optimus sensitivity, but many are Chinese or Japanese listings and few have disclosed firm Tesla purchase orders.
The rare-earth magnet opportunity should be framed as a strategic supply-chain build rather than a current humanoid commodity shortage. Humanoid demand is still tiny relative to existing global NdFeB production, while Western governments are deliberately financing mine-to-magnet capacity years ahead of expected demand.
Investment frame
The highest-value early signal is not “robot shipments are already consuming huge magnet tonnage.” It is “qualified non-China magnet capacity, feedstock and downstream manufacturing are being financed, contracted and reserved before physical-AI demand is material.”

3. Optimus bill of materials and recurring / consumable exposure
Actuators, screws and precision reducers — strongest mechanical opportunity
Tesla job descriptions provide a practical bill-of-materials map. Actuator engineering work references linear and rotary actuators; planetary, cycloidal and harmonic/strain-wave gearing; planetary roller screws, ball screws and lead screws; torque and force sensing; electric motors and motor controls; bearings, races, shafts, lubrication and cleanliness requirements.
Tesla is also hiring sourcing personnel to issue RFQs, negotiate tooling and piece prices, select actuator suppliers, run supplier scorecards and participate in design-for-manufacturing reviews. That indicates supplier selection and pricing remain active rather than fully settled.
The user-provided supplier map carried an estimate that actuators represent roughly 56% of Optimus BOM, with about 20 rotary + 14 linear actuators, roughly 20 harmonic reducers and 30+ screws per robot. These figures should be treated as a research-map estimate unless directly tied to Tesla documentation.
The hand — likely one of the most service-intensive assemblies
Tesla describes the hand as a compact integration of sensors, actuators, bearings, linkages, tendons, compliance elements and electrical interconnects. Tesla is explicitly testing tendon and interconnect failure modes and designing replaceable/serviceable assemblies. Potential recurring-content suppliers include tactile/force sensors, miniature motors and reducers, tendon or cable-drive materials, flexible printed circuits, miniature bearings and high-cycle connectors.
Compute, memory, PCBAs and power electronics — strongest confirmed relationships
Tesla says Optimus uses specialized power electronics and circuit boards designed internally but fabricated by outside suppliers. Robot-specific work includes metal-injection-molded parts and flexible printed circuits. AI inference-board jobs reference Tesla silicon, DDR-class memory, storage, networking and complex power electronics. Tesla has said AI5 is initially intended for Optimus.
Vertical integration is the caveat: Musk has said Optimus 4 in Austin will use a more vertically integrated supply system and suggested Tesla may bring considerable PCB work in-house. A Gen 3 supplier win therefore does not guarantee identical Gen 4 content.
Batteries, structures and factory-process consumption
Panasonic is the battery supplier Musk specifically thanked in the Optimus/robotaxi supply-chain discussion, citing billions invested in cell-production expansion. Tesla also has Optimus engineering work around connectors and high-cycle dynamic interconnect systems. The battery is an eventual replacement item, but pack size and Optimus-specific allocation are undisclosed.
Lightweight structures include actuator housings, torso/chest pieces, joints and other aluminum or magnesium components. They scale with each robot but are generally less scarce and more exposed to Tesla piece-price pressure than precision motion components.
Fremont process-engineering postings reference two-part adhesives, Atlas Copco fastening-rundown data and Keyence vision inspection/profilometry. These indicate recurring factory process consumption and equipment demand, although most value is factory capex rather than recurring robot content.
4. Precision-motion stack
The motion stack is the strongest mechanical opportunity because the parts must combine precision, force density, low backlash, low weight, durability and high-volume cost. Qualification is difficult, and a failed screw/reducer/actuator can degrade joint accuracy after millions of cycles.
Planetary roller screws: high force density and durability; potentially one of the highest-value content categories per robot.
Ball screws / miniature screws: likely important in legs, waist and smaller linear-motion assemblies.
Harmonic / strain-wave reducers: compact, low-backlash rotary motion; high precision and historically difficult to manufacture at low cost.
Cycloidal / planetary reducers: alternative architectures for high load and durability.
Integrated actuators: combine motor, gearing, screw, sensing and housing; higher dollar content but may face Tesla insourcing risk.
Bearings / races / shafts / lubrication: less glamorous but essential for service life and repeatability.
Encoders and force/torque sensors: precision feedback and safety; hand and actuator designs can create high sensor count.
5. Hands, interconnects, compute, batteries and structures
Hands and dynamic interconnects
The hand may become one of the highest lifecycle-replacement assemblies because it combines tiny actuators, tendons, bearings, sensors and high-cycle flexible wiring in a compact package. The supply chain remains opaque; no tactile-sensor or tendon supplier is publicly confirmed by Tesla.
Memory and compute
Micron has the clearest U.S.-listed allocation evidence. Musk thanked Micron for making “very significant” memory capacity available to Tesla in the years to come and described the terms as reasonable despite tight memory pricing. Samsung and TSMC are also named by Tesla as major AI-compute partners for its future chip program.
Power distribution
Robot batteries, BMS, DC/DC conversion, wiring, connectors and power distribution are recurring content categories, but Tesla’s vertical integration and existing automotive supplier base make pure Optimus attribution difficult.

9. Rumor audit and corrections
Sanhua RMB5B / ~$685M / ~180,000 robots
This is the most important correction. The exact rumor was formally denied by Sanhua. It should not be used as evidence that Tesla prepaid for or committed to 180,000 robots’ worth of actuators. Sanhua may still have genuine development/capacity exposure, but the reported order value and exclusivity are not established.
VPG tactile-sensor thesis
A widely circulated Reddit thesis argued Vishay Precision Group could become a near-monopoly force/tactile supplier to Tesla, Figure and other humanoid OEMs at several thousand dollars of sensor content per robot. The thread supplied no verifiable Tesla design win, contract or robust basis for the per-robot content estimate. Treat as D-grade speculation.
Harmonic Drive Systems, Nabtesco, Leaderdrive
Reddit and secondary discussions frequently state that Optimus uses specific reducer suppliers. HDS is investable because its own filings show real AI-robot bookings—not because social media has proven a Tesla award.
Mirle and Asia Optical
Many social posts trace back to the same Taiwanese supply-chain article rather than independent confirmation. Treat the underlying JV/factory activity as real where filed, and the Tesla designation as unconfirmed unless primary evidence appears.
10. Fremont retooling and physical commitment
The user-provided JPMorgan factory-tour research says Fremont’s discontinued Model S/X area is being converted to Optimus production, with an “Optimus Academy” deployment in 2H26, internal factory use thereafter and external sales potentially beginning in 2H27. It relays aspirational capacity targets of roughly 1 million units at Fremont and 10 million at Texas. These are production ambitions, not supplier-order forecasts.
Important calibration
Retooling former S/X space is an irreversible physical commitment but was done at relatively low opportunity cost because S/X were already discontinued/low volume. It is strong evidence that Tesla is serious about production, but weaker evidence that the ultimate volume targets will be reached. Converting a high-volume Model Y line would be a much stronger volume signal.

Tesla’s own commentary is cautious: Musk has said Optimus essentially lacks an existing supply chain, the initial manufacturing S-curve will be unusually flat and long, and Tesla must either build suppliers from scratch or insource components. Tesla filings also warn that most supplier arrangements do not create long-term binding purchase obligations.
11. Rare-earth / NdPr / NdFeB thesis
What the robot actually consumes
Do not mix magnet mass with rare-earth content. The working research carried both roughly 1.3–3.5 kg of NdFeB magnet mass per humanoid and about 0.9 kg of NdPr content. These can be directionally consistent because NdFeB magnets are roughly 30–32% Nd/Pr by weight. A 3.5 kg magnet load would therefore contain about 1.0–1.1 kg of Nd/Pr. They are different instruments, not competing estimates.
The standing disconfirmer
Humanoids are a rounding error in magnet demand today. The user-provided research counted disclosed 2025 deliveries of Unitree >5,500 plus UBTECH 1,079 = 6,579 units, excluding AGIBOT’s cumulative figure. At 3.5–4.0 kg of magnets per robot, that is only about 23–26 tonnes—roughly 0.01% of China’s ~205,000 t/yr magnet production and about 0.1% of ex-China ~23,500 t. Current NdPr price action is driven much more by EVs, wind, defense and broader industrial demand.
That means the magnet chokepoint binds when robots ship at scale, not when production lines are merely installed.
Where the early-cycle signal actually is
The cleanest March-2025-memory analogue is not generic NdPr spot price. It is qualified non-China NdFeB magnet capacity plus secured non-China feedstock. Raw NdPr itself has shown mixed signals, including periods of balanced/surplus conditions and pricing near government support levels. Downstream capacity reservations, qualification and long-term contracting are stronger signals.
MP Project Swarm lets drone and motor companies reserve future magnet capacity before their end markets reach scale.
MP’s existing Independence magnet capacity is substantially committed; Apple and GM represent major long-duration customers.
Japan has locked in substantial Lynas NdPr supply through long-term arrangements, including a reported 5,000 t/yr firm commitment and price support.
USAR says customer qualification is developing into a growing backlog of prototype and production purchase orders while Stillwater capacity ramps.
Neo Performance Materials has multi-year programs with Tier-1 motor makers and capacity-reservation structures.
China’s restrictions on heavy rare earths such as Dy/Tb demonstrate that “global supply exists” is not equivalent to “Western OEMs can reliably access qualified supply.”
NdPr versus heavy rare earths
NdPr/NdFeB is the cleaner robotics-volume exposure. Dy and Tb are exceptionally strategic for high-temperature and specialty magnets, but MP has argued that physical-AI applications may increasingly use HREE-light or HREE-free magnet designs. UUUU’s Dy/Tb exposure therefore remains highly strategic but may not scale linearly with humanoid unit volume.
12. Trump administration magnet and critical-minerals funding
Trump has explicitly highlighted magnets as a strategic need, and the capital allocation matches the rhetoric. The policy pattern is not simply “fund more mines.” It is attempting to build a complete non-China chain: mine → separated oxide → metal/alloy → finished permanent magnet → recycling, while also funding rare-earth-free substitutes.

Portfolio interpretation
MP = highest policy certainty: government shareholder/economic backstop, NdPr price support, magnet offtake and existing production.
USAR = highest government-funded capacity-growth torque: large incentives/loan relative to current operating scale, with a material share aimed at finished magnets and metals.
UUUU = high-value scarcity-node exposure: Dy/Tb/NdPr separation and metallization, with potential downstream magnet integration.
Important counterpoint: Washington is also funding rare-earth-free substitutes, so the strongest thesis is “Western qualified permanent-magnet manufacturing becomes strategically protected,” not necessarily “NdPr price goes vertical.”
13. “Next memory cycle” analogue: what to watch
What made memory useful as an analogy
Before Micron’s 2025 rerating, specialist memory headlines repeatedly shifted toward price hikes, spot-price recovery, inventory drawdown, production cuts/EOL and tight supply. The useful leading signal was not the word “DRAM” by itself; it was the combination of product + price direction + supply action.
In the working headline study, the pre-run March–April 2025 sample showed price/pricing in about half of the specialist headlines, with repeated use of price hikes, rising spot prices, inventory changes, production cuts and HBM capacity pressure. The stock moved later, after the physical signals had already turned.

15. Disconfirmers and risks
Humanoid volume risk: disclosed shipments remain tiny versus global magnet production. If robot deployment slips years, magnet demand from physical AI remains immaterial.
Policy-created overcapacity: governments are funding capacity before conventional economics prove demand. Some projects may become structurally uneconomic without support.
Substitution risk: rare-earth-free magnets (for example Niron iron-nitride) and HREE-light designs can reduce the commodity intensity of robotics demand.
China cost advantage: China retains overwhelming mining, processing and magnet-manufacturing scale. Western supply may require long-duration support to remain competitive.
Tesla insourcing risk: Optimus 4 is expected to use a more vertically integrated architecture; Gen 3 supplier wins may not persist.
Framework-order risk: a supplier can build tooling/capacity to a customer forecast without receiving a binding purchase commitment for full planned volume.
Qualification risk: magnets, screws, reducers and sensors must survive performance/durability qualification; announced capacity is not the same as qualified production.
Valuation risk: a genuine physical bottleneck can still be a poor investment if equities already discount several years of scarcity.
Raw NdPr counter-signal: balanced/surplus raw-material conditions can coexist with downstream Western magnet scarcity, so the commodity price may not validate the thesis immediately.

Tesla reserves a disclosed quantity of non-China magnet capacity.

Headline scanner terms
Product / material: NdPr, NdFeB, permanent magnet, sintered magnet, Dy, Tb, magnet alloy, magnet powder, servomotor, actuator, planetary roller screw, harmonic reducer, strain-wave gear.
Bullish cycle language: price hike, pricing power, allocation, shortage, binding constraint, reserve capacity, long-term offtake, prepayment, strategic customer agreement, qualification, sold out, lead time, stockpiling, export license, export control, capacity reservation.
Reversal language: surplus, price decline, underutilization, order cancellation, framework renegotiation, delayed ramp, customer destocking, capacity ahead of demand, substitution, rare-earth-free, qualification failure.
Final evidence-weighted conclusion
The supply-chain map is a watchlist, not a finished thesis. Tesla is physically committing to Optimus production and is deeply engaged with Chinese component suppliers, but most large order-size claims remain secondary and some have been explicitly denied. The rare-earth opportunity is strongest as a strategic Western mine-to-magnet build: MP is the clearest U.S. national-champion expression, USAR has high government-funded capacity torque, and UUUU targets scarce separation/metallization nodes. The cleanest “next memory cycle” confirmation would be a binding multi-year non-China magnet capacity agreement from Tesla or another scaled humanoid OEM before robot shipments become large.
