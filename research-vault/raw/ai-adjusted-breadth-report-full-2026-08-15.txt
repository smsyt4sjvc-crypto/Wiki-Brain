S&P 500 Breadth Report
AI-adjusted breadth, AI-capex-chain earnings, and the residual P/E falsification test
Working report assembled from the prior analysis in this conversation. Market figures are approximate point-in-time estimates and should be re-verified before publication or trading use.
Core question
Is the apparent broadening of S&P 500 leadership actually independent economic breadth, or is one AI capital-spending cycle creating winners across many sectors and simultaneously inflating the earnings denominator that makes the index look cheaper?

Decisive test
Strip earnings attributable to the AI-capex chain from index EPS while keeping the full S&P 500 price as the numerator. If the resulting residual P/E is much higher and rising while the headline P/E is flat or falling, apparent multiple compression may be largely capital-cycle earnings rather than genuine broad-market cheapening.

Baseline dates used
Breadth/performance snapshot: through August 14, 2026.
FactSet forward-earnings baseline: August 7, 2026.
Scheduled refresh: after the next FactSet Earnings Insight release on August 28, 2026.

Executive conclusion
Conventional breadth looks healthy, but economic-driver breadth is materially narrower. Many winners classified across Technology, Industrials, Utilities, Materials and parts of Financials are exposed to the same underlying AI/data-center capital-spending impulse. That means sector breadth and equal-weight participation can overstate the number of independent earnings engines supporting the market.
The stronger test is valuation. The August 7 baseline put the S&P 500 near 20.0x forward earnings. A strict first-pass strip of direct AI-capex suppliers moved the cycle-adjusted index multiple to about 24.7x; an expanded infrastructure strip put it near 25.0x. This does not prove a bubble by itself. The potentially decisive evidence is whether that residual P/E is also rising over time while the published multiple compresses.

1. Why ordinary breadth can be deceptive
A normal breadth statistic asks how many stocks are rising. The more relevant question in this market is how many independent sources of earnings growth are causing those stocks to rise.

Key distinction
Ticker breadth can be broad and sector breadth can be broad while economic-driver breadth remains concentrated. Nvidia -> Dell -> Eaton -> Quanta -> GE Vernova -> Corning may span several GICS sectors but still represent multiple layers of one capital-spending chain.

3. Industrials are a major contamination channel
Examples cited in the prior analysis included Comfort Systems (+90%), Corning (+90%), Vertiv (+81%), GE Vernova (+63%), Quanta Services (+62%), Generac (+61%), Caterpillar (+50%) and Eaton (+42%) YTD. In a sector-breadth chart these appear to validate independent industrial leadership. Economically, a meaningful portion of the incremental demand is tied to data-center construction, power, cooling, electrical systems, fiber and generation.
4. Energy should not be stripped wholesale
Energy is important counterevidence to an overly broad AI-bubble claim. Refiners, oil producers and services companies have large independent drivers: crude prices, refining margins, geopolitics, supply discipline and commodity cycles. Some natural-gas, pipeline, turbine and power-equipment demand intersects with data centers, but removing an entire sector would overstate AI dependence.
Classification rule
Do not label whole sectors as AI. Attribute only the portion of revenue, incremental EBIT, financing fees or valuation re-rating that is demonstrably linked to AI/data-center capex.

5. Financing transmission broadens the same cycle
The AI buildout is increasingly moving from self-funded hyperscaler capex into bonds, project finance, private credit and infrastructure capital. The prior FactSet-based discussion cited external debt rising from roughly 9% of hyperscaler capex in FY24 to about 32% on a trailing-twelve-month basis, with aggregate hyperscaler debt around $700 billion.
This means some apparent Financials-sector earnings growth can be a second-order derivative of the same AI investment cycle through underwriting fees, lending, structured/project finance, asset management and private credit. The correct adjustment is attributable earnings, not deleting entire banks or financial firms.

Preferred breadth measures
AI-Adjusted Winning Breadth = economically independent winners / all S&P 500 constituents.
AI-Adjusted Outperformance Breadth = economically independent companies outperforming the S&P / all S&P 500 constituents.
Marginal earnings exposure matters more than revenue exposure. A company can have modest current data-center revenue while data centers explain most incremental EBIT growth and multiple expansion.
7. The residual-EPS / P/E test
Primary formula
Cycle-adjusted S&P P/E = Full S&P 500 price / (Total S&P forward EPS - AI-capex-chain forward EPS)

This keeps the full index price as the numerator because the question is not “what P/E does the non-AI portfolio trade at?” It is “how expensive is today’s S&P 500 price if the earnings generated by an extraordinary AI capital cycle are not treated as permanent underlying earnings power?”

8. The trend test that can kill the thesis
The level test is suggestive; the trend test is decisive. From June 30 to August 7 in the prior FactSet comparison:
Metric
June 30 -> Aug. 7 change
S&P 500 price
+2.8%
Forward 12-month S&P EPS
+4.7%
Headline forward P/E
20.4x -> 20.0x

For the residual P/E to have risen over that interval, residual EPS needed to grow by less than the 2.8% rise in price. Under the approximate 19% starting earnings share used above, AI-capex-chain companies would have needed to account for roughly 52% or more of the total increase in forward S&P EPS during the period.
Critical threshold
If AI-capex-chain revisions explain >= ~52% of the June 30-to-Aug. 7 increase in forward index EPS, the residual P/E would have risen even though the published forward P/E fell from 20.4x to 20.0x.

That is the test to reconstruct with point-in-time constituent estimates. Commentary cited in the prior analysis suggested AI-infrastructure beneficiaries may be contributing roughly half or more of aggregate S&P earnings growth, but earnings-growth contribution is not identical to the change in point-in-time forward EPS estimates. The exact historical series still needs to be computed.

10. Recommended monthly series
For each month-end, use point-in-time constituent forward EPS rather than current estimates backfilled historically. Compute:
Total S&P forward EPS.
Minus direct AI compute / semiconductors / memory / networking earnings.
Minus semiconductor equipment earnings.
Minus attributable server, storage and optical-connectivity earnings.
Minus attributable data-center power, cooling, electrical and construction earnings.
Minus attributable AI-related credit, underwriting and project-finance earnings where measurable.
Residual forward EPS and full-index price / residual EPS.
A hard-strip series (100% of direct suppliers) and an attributable-strip series (only the estimated AI/data-center share for diversified firms).
11. Scheduled update criteria
A scheduled refresh is set for August 28, 2026, after the next FactSet Earnings Insight release. The update should report:
Headline forward S&P 500 P/E and implied forward index EPS.
Strict direct AI-capex supplier strip.
Expanded AI/data-center infrastructure strip.
AI-capex share of forward S&P earnings.
Residual S&P forward EPS and cycle-adjusted residual P/E.
Change versus the August 7 baseline: ~20.0x headline and ~24.7–25.0x AI-stripped.
Most important: whether residual P/E is rising while headline P/E is flat or falling, and whether that strengthens or weakens the capital-cycle/bubble hypothesis.
12. Bottom line
Conventional S&P breadth: healthy.
Independent economic breadth: materially weaker than conventional breadth suggests.
Top-tail leadership: heavily dominated by direct AI and AI-infrastructure beneficiaries.
Financial-system dependence: increasing as capex migrates toward bonds, project finance and private credit.
Residual valuation: the first-pass level test suggests roughly 24.7–25.0x versus about 20.0x headline.
Decisive unresolved question: whether the residual P/E is rising over time while the reported multiple compresses.
Working thesis
One trade can masquerade as breadth when it creates earnings across semiconductors, servers, industrial equipment, power, construction, materials and finance. The residual-EPS P/E series is the cleanest way to determine whether headline multiple compression is genuine or primarily the consequence of a capital-cycle earnings surge.
