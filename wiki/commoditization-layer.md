# The commoditisation layer — who captures value when intelligence becomes a commodity

*Opened 2026-10-05 ~5:30pm PDT (filed under rule 22c) on Jake's ask: "We really need to get a better read on who's gonna win that commoditization layer. It all points to Nvidia, MU and chips, but they've already run so hard. There's gotta be a software integration layer." One idea: the SEATS between the commoditising model layer and the end buyer, which are priced already, and which have a falsifiable test. Lineage: the routing/orchestration thread opened 7/25 ([[metered-compute]] L617-655: "the router is the commoditisation engine; the durable seat is evals; take-rate is the contested variable; NOW = the layer-4 expression; DDOG the only public evals name; clouds own enterprise routing"); the 10/5 token-economics day ([[compression-thesis]] 9:30am-4:15pm).*

**WHY THIS MATTERS (plain English):** when the thing being sold becomes a commodity, the money moves to whoever stands between the commodity and the customer: the exchange, the pipe, the quality-checker, the contract-holder. The chips are the obvious winner and the market has paid them. The question is which software seat gets paid next, and whether it is already priced.

## THESIS (interpretation — NOT fact)
- *(analysis — the answer, first line)* **The software integration layer that is MEASURABLE has already run as hard as the chips: DDOG +107% and NET +78% YTD against SOXX +88%, all within 4% of their highs. The seat that has NOT run is the workflow/data layer, down 25-60% from highs on the "AI eats seats" discount — and that is the seat a strategic buyer just paid 42% for.** The vault's 7/25 ranking (NOW first as the layer-4 seat, DDOG under-ranked, NET adjacency-not-toll, clouds own enterprise routing) survives the day with one change: DDOG and NET are no longer under-ranked by the market.
- *(analysis — why the workflow seat is the contested one)* The compression thesis says seat-based software margin compresses; the 10/5 evidence says the DATA under the seat does not (Meta restricts Codex to keep competitor code out of its training set; Schneider pays for PTC's CAD/PLM records; NOW meters Now Assist consumption against a pool). The split inside the seat is therefore: **names whose revenue converts from seats to metered consumption on proprietary data survive the commoditisation; names selling seats on commodity data do not.** NOW has the conversion datum ($750M ACV, ≥$1.5B target, pools + overage); ADSK/PTC/INTU own data but have not shown the conversion (PTC's buyer is paying for the data, not the seats).
- *(analysis — the marketplace seat is the cleanest "wins whichever model wins")* Microsoft lost Claude Code users internally and gained the same workloads on Copilot, while its customers' Anthropic consumption on Azure rose — the same company is paid on both sides of the substitution. It is the carrier in the telecom map (volume, not margin), and the market has not priced it for AI (+9% YTD) because the AI revenue is still "minimal" from the third-party shelf. The HSBC $43B is a 2030 number (rule 16c(c): outside the window).
- *(analysis — the evals seat is right and paid)* DDOG is the only public evals/observability name and its own telemetry (69% multi-model, 5% failures) is the demand case; at +107% YTD and iv ⬜ it is a hold-the-thesis, not an entry, until a pullback or a product-level disclosure; NET sells the gateway to sell security around it (adjacency) and does not segment AI revenue — the $500M run-rate is the one number, unaudited by product.
- *(user's thesis — Jake, 7/25 and 8/27)* NOW = the integration layer / "agentic CFO" of the NVDA stack (forest 8/27: the control-plane receipt). **Independence: MEDIUM-HIGH** (derived months before the Street's "execution layer" framing; the 10/5 consumption datum is new support). **Steelman against:** ~~NOW −50% in a year~~ ⛔ corrected 10/5 ~5:20pm at proofread: NOW is −26% over one year and −29% from its 1y high (`tape.py`, closes to 10/2) — still says the market has decided seats compress faster than consumption grows; a $1.5B Now Assist ACV is ~12% of a ~$13B revenue base — the conversion has to outrun the seat erosion, and the 4/9 bundling may be cannibalising list price to show the ACV.
- *(disconfirmers, named)* (1) The private routers could become public (OpenRouter's cap table: Alphabet, Nvidia; Stripe talks) and take the take-rate the vault says is thin — if a listed router shows a durable bps take, the "pipe is thin" read is wrong. (2) The hyperscalers could absorb evals/observability natively (Azure AI Foundry evaluations, Bedrock guardrails), compressing DDOG's seat. (3) A model vendor could vertically absorb routing (OpenAI's Luna/Sol/Astra ladder is routing by the vendor), leaving no independent seat at all.
