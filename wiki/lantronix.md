# Lantronix (LTRX): the listed drone-compute supplier

A supplier of Qualcomm-based onboard computing modules ("Open-Q") into many drone and counter-drone makers. The
vault cares because it is one of the few LISTED ways to get paid when Pentagon drone money goes to PRIVATE
airframe and autonomy firms ([[new-economy-regime]] 10/1 and 10/4: the drone dollars keep landing on privates).

## THESIS (interpretation, NOT fact)
- **(analysis) The business turn is real and measured:** unmanned came in above its own outlook, margins rose,
  operating cash flow rose, and the drone book has more engagements and named listed customers (SWMR). As a
  supplier into many platforms, it gets paid whichever private drone maker wins, which the vault's
  "money goes to privates" finding says is where the awards are landing.
- **(analysis) THE PRICE PROBLEM — the stock is back at the price where the company sells stock.** The May offering
  priced at ≈$7.19 and the ATM averaged ≈$7.24; the 10/2 close is $7.19 with ~$17M of ATM capacity left. A
  company that raised twice at this level has every reason to keep selling here ⇒ **a supply overhang right at
  the current price.** The 17% share growth already happened; per-share earnings must outrun it.
- **(analysis) The CEO buy is a $5.18 signal on a $7.19 stock.** It says management thought ~$5 was cheap, not
  that $7.19 is. Small ($78K) against a 453K-share holding.
- **(analysis) The move already happened:** +36% in under four weeks while the listed drone peers fell. The
  re-rating has run ahead of the next datum.
- **Steelman (the bull case under-weighted here):** a supplier with >30 engagements and design-ins across
  airframes, autonomy software and payloads can compound once its modules are qualified (NDAA/TAA-compliant,
  10-year lifecycle): customers rarely re-qualify compute mid-program. If Q1 shows unmanned ≥$5M in a quarter, the
  FY27 range moves to its top and ~52x falls fast.
- **Independence score:** medium — the paste and the release share one origin (management's framing); filings
  verify the numbers, not the "go-to edge compute company" claim.
