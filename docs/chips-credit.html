Wiki-Brain · AI demand vs AI financing · since CoreWeave's IPO (3/28/2025)

Chip demand is still climbing. Credit stress is catching up, and only for the borrowers without the cash.

credit through 2026-10-14 · TSMC revenue through Sep 2026 · CDS through 2026-10-14 · built 2026-10-15 11:15am PT
Is chip demand flattening?
No. It sped up.

TSMC revenue grew 35% from a year earlier in Q1 2026, 36% in Q2 and 51% in Q3 (in Taiwan dollars; +41% in US dollars). September was flat on August (−0.6%), which is one month. TSMC guided Q4 up another 12–16% on Q3, in US dollars.

Is the spread flattening?
Demand vs stress: yes, fast.

Through September demand pulled away from credit stress: the gap between the lines in Chart 1 reached 48 points. Junk spreads then went from a September average of 1,086bp to 1,282bp on 10/14, which cuts the gap to about 25 if demand held at September's level. Credit is catching up; demand has not slowed. Between the two kinds of borrower it is the opposite: that spread is widening (Chart 4).

Are rates driving the stress?
For the giants, yes. For CoreWeave, no.

Since the IPO the investment-grade borrowing rate rose 0.79 points, and all of it came from Treasury yields: their credit spread actually fell. The junk rate rose 4.79 points, 82% of it credit spread. Since 9/30 the 10-year yield fell (5.29% → 5.21% on 10/13) while junk spreads widened by 103bp.

TSMC monthly revenue, NT$ billions

Chart 3 · are rates doing it?

What raised each tier's borrowing rate

Treasury part (pale) Credit-spread part (solid)

Each bar is the change in the index's all-in yield, split into the Treasury part (yield minus spread) and the credit-spread part. The split uses each index's own yield and spread, so it is matched to the index's maturity rather than to the 10-year. Day to day, junk spreads and the 10-year have not moved together: the correlation of daily changes is -0.15 since the IPO (n=385 days; weakly opposite, so spreads tended to widen on days yields fell) and +0.07 since August 1 (n=49; no relationship).

Chart 4 · the two borrowers, on their own credit

Five-year credit default swaps since 9/28, indexed to 9/28 = 100

CoreWeave Cash-flow four: Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta (average) Oracle

A credit default swap (CDS) is insurance against a borrower defaulting; its price in basis points (bp) is the market's fear gauge for that one company. Since 9/28: CoreWeave 858 → 1,117bp (+259), Oracle 251 → 286bp (+35), the cash-flow four 75.3 → 77.9bp (+2.6). CoreWeave's premium over the four went from 782 to 1,039bp. Between the two borrower types the spread is widening, not flattening.

So where does the money go

  • Cash-flow giants
    GOOGL · AMZN · MSFT · META    Their higher borrowing cost is a rates story, and their own credit barely moved (+2.6bp in CDS while CoreWeave's rose 259bp). A bond rally helps them directly. This is the case for holding GOOGL through the chip sell-off.
  • Debt-funded borrowers
    CRWV · ORCL    Theirs is a credit story, and lower Treasury yields do not reach them: since 9/30 the 10-year fell while their spreads blew out. That is the CRWV short.
  • The suppliers
    TSM · the chip names    Orders were still accelerating through September. The risk is whether the debt-funded buyers can keep paying, not end demand. TSM fell on a record quarter, so the market is pricing the financing, not the orders.

What this cannot see

  • Charts 1 and 3 use bond indexes (ICE BofA CCC and investment-grade) as stand-ins, not each company's own bonds. CoreWeave's 2032 notes yield about 16.1% to worst (reported 10/15), a little below the CCC index's 17.6% and far above the investment-grade index's 5.9%, so the market prices CoreWeave close to the CCC tier.
  • Company CDS only starts 8/21/2026, with a gap from 8/24 to 9/28 and a contract roll on 9/20. Only the current (Dec-2031) contract is plotted. The bp figures are model conversions of upfront prices: read the changes, not the levels.
  • The "gap" in Chart 1 is a picture, not a measured quantity: revenue and spreads are different units, put on one scale by indexing. October has no TSMC month yet (due ~11/10).
  • TSMC revenue is in Taiwan dollars and includes price increases and non-AI chips.
  • The 10-year is FRED's daily close through 2026-10-13.
